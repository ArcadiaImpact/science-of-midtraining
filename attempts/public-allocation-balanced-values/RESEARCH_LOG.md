# Research log: balanced contrastive values-and-rationales

This attempt followed the corrected public allocation experiment in #424 and the ethical-style-only extension in #436. #424 found no robust evidence that positive values-and-rationales midtraining increased rationale/behavior decoupling relative to rules or matched irrelevant training. #436 showed that ethical vocabulary alone strongly raised the detached lexical training proxy, yet did not raise the joint false-aligned-violation interaction. I therefore tested a distinct semantics hypothesis: perhaps balanced public contrast—explicitly diagnosing a wrong but feasible action and immediately correcting it—would help the policy discriminate faithful ethical reasoning from merely ethical-sounding language during later rationale-only reinforcement learning.

Before any paid call, I built and inspected 36 exactly token-matched documents for a Qwen3.6-27B policy. Eighteen are #424's positive compliant values-and-rationales examples. Eighteen display the full public state and objective, a feasible second-ranked allocation labeled NONCOMPLIANT with deterministic consequence arithmetic, then the oracle allocation labeled COMPLIANT with a faithful need-based reason and an instruction to return only the correction. The audit verified the 18/18 split, every action and consequence, exact 10,542-token matching, zero prohibited terms, the dense-27B canary, and that the reinforcement reward receives only extracted public rationale text. I then trained three fresh SDF states and nine fresh eight-step rationale-only RL trajectories spanning action-first, rationale-first, and detached two-pass generation. One detached service request was slow for several minutes, but it completed without retry; no trajectory was replaced.

The independent surface judge passed its 96-anchor calibration perfectly (sensitivity 1.0, false-positive rate 0, AUC 1.0), and the minimum endpoint feasible-allocation rate was 0.806. Balanced contrastive training increased the rationale-first joint false-aligned-violation checkpoint change by 0.083 relative to irrelevant training in every seed (seed interactions 0.056, 0.083, and 0.111). This was entirely an action effect: the oracle-violation interaction was also 0.083 and conditional surface alignment did not change. Relative to positive-only values-and-rationales, detached two-pass generation showed a 0.083 joint interaction in every seed (0.139, 0.056, and 0.056), decomposed into a 0.028 mean oracle-violation interaction and a 0.066 conditional surface-alignment interaction. The detached-versus-irrelevant interaction was less stable across seeds (mean 0.074; seed values 0.222, -0.083, and 0.083). Public counterfactual action responsiveness remained high (0.861 to 1.0), while correct compliance on those changed cases remained low.

This is evidence that the balanced corpus changed the post-hoc coupling measurement, but not a clean causal claim that ethical teaching created a facade. Half the corpus necessarily exposes the policy to wrong action tokens, even though it explicitly rejects them; that may weaken action selection independently of contrastive ethical reasoning. The task also has a high baseline joint rate—every balanced trajectory already exceeded the preregistered 0.25 threshold at checkpoint 0—so time-to-threshold is ceilinged. A useful next experiment would keep the same correction semantics while replacing the rejected allocation with a symbolic error description that never prints its action tokens, or compare against a matched nonethical contrastive-correction corpus. These findings concern observable rationale/action coupling only, not intent, deception, evaluator awareness, or concealment.
