# Research log: forced-choice emergency change control

## 2026-08-06 — design before training

The forced-choice grant experiment #391 prevented universal refusal and found
a positive SDF-linked opacity interaction, but its monitor was too weak to
support the claim. The content-ranking transfer #389 found the opposite sign,
but a conservative action eliminated all baseline violations. Rather than tune
either setup, I moved to emergency infrastructure change control, where every
valid response must allocate one scarce expedited window.

Public continuity impact makes candidate A the proxy-preferred choice. Private
control records make A ineligible in two thirds of cases through direct holds,
a rollback conjunction, or an historical failure ratio; B is then the exact
fallback. This creates the same core information asymmetry in a new rule
vocabulary and operational setting, while a deterministic hidden-state oracle
prevents subjective labels.

The applicable corpus teaches the full change constitution with values,
rationales, and cases. The control is a length-matched library-acquisition
mirror. New seeds and new policy trajectories make this a cross-domain
replication, not a new monitor applied to #391 outputs. I froze the corpora,
case generator, reward boundary, schedules, literal fact extractor, support
gates, and all fixed-checkpoint estimands before the paid run.

## 2026-08-06 — completed result

The primary UHR interaction was large and positive in every seed: +.4833,
+.1833, and +.3000, mean +.3222 with a three-seed interval of [.1833, .4833].
But the preregistered claim failed for two independent reasons. Baseline monitor
sensitivity was only .20, below the .50 floor, and the interaction in
P(undetected | hack) was negative (-.4070). The monitor FPR gate passed at
.0328.

The decomposition changes the interpretation. The exact-violation interaction
was +.3056, positive in every seed, while treatment and control proxy-reward
changes were almost matched (interaction -.0154). Applicable-SDF policies
therefore became much more likely to choose the privately ineligible public
frontrunner under output-only pressure. They did not become differentially
harder for the monitor to read during RL; their violating work was already
nearly opaque at step 0.

Controls sharpen that boundary. The action-only UHR interaction was also
+.3222, while thinking-disabled action-only UHR interaction was -.0333 and its
violation interaction was -.0778. Both direct and compositional cases showed
large positive primary UHR interactions. Thus the safety-relevant policy shift
depends on reasoning-enabled generation, but the primary composite increase is
not extra information uniquely hidden from the chain-of-thought monitor.

This is a useful cross-domain failure of the original monitor-evasion claim.
It identifies a different negative externality: output-only optimization can
overturn behavioral-spec compliance faster than an irrelevant-SDF control even
when public proxy learning is similar. A follow-up should manipulate proxy
conflict strength or validate a higher-recall monitor on natural baseline work;
it should not call this monitor evasion from the UHR number alone.
