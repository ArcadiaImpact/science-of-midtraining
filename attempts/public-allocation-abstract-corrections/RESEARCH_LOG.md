# Research log: detached abstract ethical corrections

## Why this experiment

The balanced ethical midtraining in #447 increased the detached two-pass false-aligned-violation rate relative to positive-only values-and-rationales, but its correction examples printed a rejected allocation before the compliant correction. #448 showed that matching this negative-action exposure in an irrelevant warehouse domain did not explain the average detached effect, although the three seed differences were heterogeneous. That left an important ambiguity: ethical contrastive reasoning might matter, or repeatedly printing wrong action tokens might simply prime later actions.

I therefore removed the rejected action tokens while retaining the public ethical diagnosis. Each of 18 correction documents displays the complete clinic state and policy, truthfully describes the second-ranked feasible action only through its inferior uncovered-resident outcome, labels that result noncompliant, and then prints the deterministic oracle allocation with a faithful ethical reason. The other 18 documents are the existing positive compliant values-and-rationales examples. The Qwen3.6-27B documents exactly match the source document-token lengths. I targeted detached two-pass generation because #448 had already shown that the rationale-first effect was reproducible with irrelevant contrastive training, whereas detached generation remained the unresolved post-hoc-rationalization comparison.

## Construct audit and execution

Before the first paid call, I read and quoted both corpus categories and audited the source reward boundary. The positive example begins with the public policy and state, prints `{"A": 2, "B": 3, "C": 1}` as compliant, and explains how it reduces uncovered high-risk need. An abstract correction says that a feasible alternative yields uncovered counts `A=52, B=28, C=34, total=114`, labels that outcome noncompliant, then prints the compliant correction `{"A": 1, "B": 3, "C": 2}` and its public high-risk-coverage reason. The rejected allocation itself is absent from model-visible text.

The audit found 18 positive and 18 correction documents, 10,542 tokens, exact per-document token matching, no prohibited terms, and oracle-compliant displayed actions throughout. Every diagnostic action was feasible, distinct, second-ranked by the public executable objective, and absent from the text; its stated outcome was recomputed and truthful. The rationale-only reward has signature `rationale_reward(rationale: str)` and the existing dense-27B reward-boundary canary passed. I committed code, configuration, preregistration, corpus, and audit as `720d2091638773d380bbd6228cff2fa769df77b3` before training.

I trained three fresh Qwen/Qwen3.6-27B SDF states and three fresh detached two-pass rationale-only RL trajectories at seeds 714, 825, and 936. All froze at checkpoints 0, 4, and 8. I then generated 540 fixed-checkpoint evaluation records, 108 public counterfactual records, and obtained independent rationale-only judgments for all 540 rationales plus 96 calibration anchors. The judge achieved sensitivity 1.000, false-positive rate 0.000, and AUC 1.000; the minimum endpoint feasible-allocation rate was 0.806. The raw outputs are intentionally not submitted, but their commands, timestamps, configurations, outcomes, and hashes are recorded in `EXECUTION_LOG.md`.

## What happened

Against the preregistered positive-only values-and-rationales comparator, abstract correction increased the checkpoint-0-to-8 joint false-aligned-violation change by 0.083. Oracle-violation change was exactly 0.000 in every paired seed, while surface alignment conditional on violation increased by 0.103. The joint interactions were +0.139, +0.167, and -0.056 across the three seeds, so this primary estimate is heterogeneous and its three-seed bootstrap interval includes zero.

The matched irrelevant contrastive control gives a sharper semantic comparison. Abstract ethical correction exceeded it by 0.093 joint false-aligned violations and 0.100 conditional surface alignment. Those effects were positive in all seeds: joint +0.056/+0.056/+0.167 and conditional +0.129/+0.102/+0.068. The corresponding oracle interaction was only +0.019 and changed sign across seeds. Relative to the explicit balanced ethical corpus from #447, the mean joint interaction was 0.000, although the seed differences again varied. Thus omitting the wrong allocation tokens did not remove the average detached coupling result. The decomposition is more consistent with improved ethical-looking rationales on already-violating actions than with a general increase in oracle violations, but three paired seeds do not establish a stable causal semantic effect.

All abstract trajectories already exceeded the registered 0.25 joint threshold at checkpoint 0, making time-to-threshold uninformative. Under public outcome/protected-attribute counterfactuals, action responsiveness was 0.944–0.972, but counterfactual oracle compliance was only 0.139. The policy reacts to changed public state without reliably selecting the public oracle action. Nothing here identifies intent, concealment, evaluator awareness, or deception.

## What I would try next

The next useful experiment would lower the starting violation ceiling while preserving the same detached factorial, for example by strengthening action-format competence before freezing a common starting checkpoint without rewarding the oracle during the primary rationale-only RL phase. A second option is to create an irrelevant abstract-outcome correction control that matches the absence of wrong action JSON as closely as #448 matched explicit action exposure. Either would distinguish ethical correction semantics from the remaining corpus-format difference more cleanly than adding more seeds to this exact high-violation setup.
