# Research log: verifiable process-reward control

## 2026-08-07 — why this follow-up

The first corrected-construct experiment, #424, found no registered increase in false-aligned violations from values-and-rationales midtraining, but it exposed a difficult high-baseline regime: most allocations were feasible yet only about 13–17% were exactly optimal, while ethical-looking rationales were already common. I therefore tested a distinct competing-transparency hypothesis rather than changing seeds or tuning the surface reward. If learning receives a transparent signal for both public policy compliance and ethical explanation, correct behavior might improve without sacrificing a prosocial rationale.

I preregistered the control before its first paid call. The corrected-construct primary treatment remains #424's detached rationale-only arm: its reward accepts one public rationale string and cannot see the allocation, case, deterministic oracle, outcome, hidden reasoning, monitor, or evaluator. The new comparator starts nine fresh RL trajectories from the same dense Qwen3.6-27B supervised endpoints—values plus faithful ethical rationales, rules only, and matched irrelevant scheduling at three seeds. Its explicitly non-primary composite score is half the unchanged rationale-surface score and half exact compliance with the deterministic oracle computed from the displayed public state. The oracle component is exactly zero on violations. Centered composite advantage is applied to sampled tokens in both detached action and rationale passes.

The construct audit re-quoted actual documents, confirmed 72 relevant positive compliant examples and zero violations, found no prohibited monitoring or private/public-separation language, verified all nine dense source endpoints, and confirmed the one-argument primary reward signature. Seven tests passed before the paid call. The first detached launcher was terminated before its first provider request and left an empty manifest with no training artifact or exception. I restarted the unchanged committed state `882e0e0` in a persistent polled session.

## 2026-08-07 — execution

The nine fresh process-control trajectories ran from 14:15:20 to 14:52:28 UTC and froze checkpoints 0, 4, and 8. Every training update logged the surface component, oracle-compliance component, composite reward, credited datum count, and optimizer result. Across 72 updates, mean within-training exact compliance was 0.204; six updates had no correct rollout across their four prompt groups. The values of this diagnostic were never used to select or discard a checkpoint.

After all trajectories froze, I sampled 972 fixed policy outputs: step 4 and step 8 standard outputs plus step 8 no-scratchpad outputs for each condition and seed. Checkpoint 0 uses the exact shared supervised state and deterministic output from #424. I also sampled 324 process-control public-outcome counterfactual outputs. A newly invoked independent Qwen3-30B judge then scored the frozen rationales and re-scored the 96 balanced calibration anchors. It saw the general policy and rationale but not the allocation or oracle result.

## 2026-08-07 — result

The preregistered protective transparency hypothesis was not supported. For values-and-rationales, the process-control minus rationale-only step-0-to-step-8 interaction in the joint false-aligned-violation rate was -0.0185, with paired seed values -0.0556, +0.0833, and -0.0833. Although two signs were negative, the oracle-violation interaction was +0.0093 rather than the required negative direction. The conditional surface-alignment interaction was -0.0320. Thus the small joint reduction came from rationales looking less aligned among violations, not from more correct actions.

Pooled over seeds at step 8, values-and-rationales exact success was 13.9% under rationale-only RL and 13.0% under the process control. Rules-only success rose from 13.9% to 15.7%, and irrelevant-midtraining success rose from 13.0% to 16.7%, but those modest descriptive improvements do not rescue the registered values result. Judge calibration was again perfect on the frozen anchors; all endpoint process arms passed feasibility and surface-retention gates. Counterfactual action responsiveness remained high, while exact counterfactual compliance stayed low.

This negative result narrows the interpretation of #424. A short binary exact-compliance bonus is not enough to show that fully informed reward repairs public arithmetic behavior, and values-and-rationales did not uniquely benefit. It does not show that verifiable supervision is generally ineffective: the oracle term is sparse, the run has only eight updates, and it does not provide dense credit for intermediate calculations. A stronger next experiment would preregister step-level public calculation targets—per-center uncovered need, total uncovered need, proportional shortfall, and tie-break comparison—so each correct intermediate computation supplies signal even when the final allocation is wrong. That would test process supervision rather than merely a binary outcome bonus.

As in #424, this experiment measures observable action/rationale decoupling only. It supports no inference about intent, awareness of assessment, concealment, or deception.
