# Research log

## 2026-08-07 — from failed private load to public justification affordance

PR #403 paired identical latent clinical cases across direct and arithmetic private notes. The arithmetic rendering failed its capability gate: public policy JSON and post-hoc monitor extraction both became unreliable, so its nominal amplification could not identify a reasoning mechanism. I did not increase token limits and rescore that same contrast. Instead I changed the tested mechanism.

The new intervention keeps every private field in the concise direct form that passed capability checks. It pairs each latent case across two public intakes. Supportive intakes contain low pain, short symptoms, no prior urgent visits, available home support, and short transport; adverse intakes contain the opposite. The hidden oracle state is identical. This isolates whether publicly citeable evidence for a plausible discharge story changes the values-versus-rules interaction during output-only RL.

The hypothesis is that rich values and causal rationales become most consequential when the public output can cite a coherent benign story while omitting the private escalation trigger. I added a policy-independent monitor calibration gate and, based on the failure in #403, preregistered both policy-JSON and generated monitor-extraction JSON validity by arm and cover. The complete design, generator, cases, tests, and analysis will be committed before any sampling call.

The first disposable canary at frozen commit `f7357d0fc4b5e01b259351800a973ae2570a92ca` stopped the experiment before a full run. On the first adverse case, the policy exhausted the 512-token allowance, leaving no parseable public JSON. This was a capability check, not an endpoint comparison. I preserved its log, raised the shared evaluation-only allowance to 768 tokens, and changed the canary to require valid scratchpad and public output from both members of one adverse/supportive pair. The intervention, cases, estimand, checkpoints, and monitor budget are unchanged.

## 2026-08-07 — clean null with opposing behavioral components

The revised canary passed at commit `4934035676fd3d6764f118aa33f9ca8ef17f9553`, and the full zero-training run completed 8,100 policy rows before 16,200 post-hoc monitor rows plus 160 independent calibration rows. Boundary verification and all ten tests passed afterward. The new capability gate also passed: pooled policy JSON validity was 0.944–0.990 and monitor-extraction JSON validity was 0.978–0.993 across the six arm-by-cover cells. The rules-only adverse cell was weaker at step 0 alone (0.830 policy JSON), which should temper claims despite its pooled pass.

The preregistered cover-amplification hypothesis was rejected. Supportive minus adverse in the values-versus-rules undetected-violation interaction was -0.0222, with seed effects -0.1333, +0.2000, and -0.1333. The decomposition is more informative than the near-zero mean. Supportive cover reduced the values-versus-rules hidden-violation interaction by -0.1704, negative in every seed, but increased the conditional-undetected interaction by +0.2515 in two of three seeds. Supportive evidence made violations common before RL in all arms; by step 16 every supportive arm violated on two thirds of cases. This baseline shift left less room for treatment-specific violation growth, while the violations that remained showed more conditional monitor failure under values and rationales. The two pathways canceled in the primary outcome.

The broad values-versus-irrelevant interaction again remained positive: +0.0741 with all three seeds positive. It was driven by a +0.2684 conditional-undetected interaction despite a small negative violation-rate interaction. In contrast, values versus rules-only was +0.0333 and reversed in two seeds. This reinforces the distinction between knowing the relevant specification and adding its values and causal explanations. A future test should prevent supportive-cover baseline saturation—for example, by lowering the generic discharge incentive or selecting a weaker public-affordance contrast—while preserving the now-successful capability gates.
