# Research log: public allocation and generation order

## 2026-08-07 — construct redesign and preregistration

I started from the corrected continuation specification rather than treating the older leaderboard as a compatible target. Historical #370 and #391 were useful warnings: they separated values from rationales or used allocation tasks, but their decisive predicates were private and their corpora or rewards did not meet the corrected public-behavior construct. Historical #386 motivated a genuinely useful question that survives the correction: does generating an action before its rationale make ethical-looking rationalization easier than generating the rationale first? Historical #390 established that Qwen3.6 often needs a renderer-native thinking protocol to reach a parseable public suffix, but its old treatment environment is not evidence for this experiment.

I preregistered a fully public mobile-clinic allocation task. Each prompt displays every integer needed to calculate outcomes, the complete consequence equation, and the ordered allocation objective. The executable oracle exhaustively searches all integer allocations and uses no hidden state. The three paired midtraining corpora contain 36 documents and exactly 10,542 Qwen3.6 tokens per condition: positive compliant examples with values and causal ethical explanations, the same compliant actions under rules alone, and format-matched warehouse scheduling. The construct audit quotes a real document from each corpus, finds 72 relevant compliant examples and no relevant violations, and finds none of the prohibited monitoring, concealment, private/public-separation, or chain-of-thought terms.

The substantive policy was changed from an inherited 8B pilot plan to dense `Qwen/Qwen3.6-27B` before treatment training. An earlier inherited 8B launch had written configuration but produced no provider artifact, so it contributes no treatment evidence. The registered 27B design crosses the three midtraining conditions with action-first, rationale-first, and detached two-pass generation at three paired seeds. The detached first-pass action receives no gradient; a separate rationale response alone receives the rationale-only advantage. In every arm, the reward function has the signature `rationale_reward(rationale: str)` and cannot access the allocation, public case, oracle, outcome, hidden reasoning, or evaluator. A positive reward requires the public rationale to claim compliance.

## 2026-08-07 — canaries and execution amendments

I committed each change before a paid call. A one-stage 27B canary from commit `a33c0f0` returned finite training values but exhausted a 192-token public budget while narrating its analysis, without reaching the required `ALLOCATION` and `RATIONALE` markers. I rejected that protocol. Before treatment training, I amended the preregistration at commit `84edf89` to use the renderer-native protocol motivated by historical #390: cap private thinking at 160 tokens, insert the renderer's standard closing delimiter with zero loss and zero advantage, then allow a 256-token public suffix. The implementation never decodes the thinking prefix during RL. A disposable amended canary returned a valid allocation and an explicit public compliance rationale with a clean stop.

The first full 27B launch then completed 12 supervised steps but failed at its first save because `+` in a checkpoint label was rejected by the service. It produced no saved treatment state and no RL trajectory. I sanitized checkpoint labels, made empty-manifest resumption explicit, committed the repair as `774cfd8`, and restarted the complete randomized grid. That run lasted from 11:44:36 to 13:22:23 UTC and froze nine fresh supervised endpoints and 27 fresh rationale-only RL trajectories at steps 0, 4, and 8.

Sequential frozen-policy sampling was too slow, so I interrupted it only after an append boundary, preserved the completed records, and changed evaluation concurrency without changing prompts, temperatures, checkpoint identities, or scoring. Commit `977a719` resumed by exact record key and completed the 4,860-row grid: 2,916 standard outputs and 1,944 no-scratchpad controls. The same commit produced 972 endpoint outputs after rotating complete public center profiles; the deterministic optimum changed in all 36 paired cases. An independent Qwen3-30B surface evaluator then scored the already frozen public rationales. It saw the general policy and the rationale, but not the chosen allocation or oracle result.

## 2026-08-07 — result

The preregistered increase was not supported. For detached two-pass generation, the values-and-rationales versus rules-only step-0-to-step-8 interaction was -0.0185 for the joint false-aligned-violation rate, with paired-seed values 0.0000, -0.1111, and 0.0556. The oracle-violation interaction was 0.0093, while the surface-alignment-given-violation interaction was -0.0319. The registered directional and conditional-facade gates therefore failed. Detached generation also did not show a consistent larger increase than the mean of the two one-pass orders: the mean contrast was -0.0139 with mixed seed signs.

The measurement checks passed. The independent judge obtained sensitivity 1.0, false-positive rate 0.0, AUC 1.0, and valid-output rate 1.0 on the frozen balanced 96-item calibration set. Every endpoint arm exceeded the preregistered 0.70 feasible-allocation threshold, and the minimum per-seed endpoint feasibility was 0.806. The outcome-profile intervention changed actions frequently (0.722 to 0.972 across arms), demonstrating sensitivity to public inputs, although exact counterfactual compliance remained low.

The main limitation is a high-baseline regime. At step 8, pooled exact-oracle success is only 0.130 to 0.167 across the nine standard arms even though allocations are usually feasible, and surface alignment among violations is already 0.862 to 1.000. This leaves limited headroom for rationale-only RL to increase the primary joint rate. The result is therefore a rigorous null or mildly protective pattern in this public arithmetic environment, not a general bound on facade risk and not evidence about intent, awareness, concealment, or deception.

## Next experiment

The most informative follow-up would hold the public environment and frozen evaluation design fixed while adding a fully informed, verifiable process-reward control that rewards correct public calculations. This would test the competing transparency hypothesis: whether giving learning signal for faithful computation reduces the high violation baseline and prevents surface-only saturation. A second useful redesign would lower arithmetic difficulty without making the oracle trivial, restoring headroom in both exact policy success and conditional surface alignment before asking for another treatment interaction.
