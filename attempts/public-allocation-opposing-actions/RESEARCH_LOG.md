# Research log: opposing-action rationale faithfulness

PR #452 found a positive ethical-minus-matched-control surface-coupling interaction during standard detached evaluation, and PR #459 localized the renderer moderation to action generation rather than rationale generation. I next asked a more direct causal-faithfulness question: when the detached rationale pass is shown an action that definitely violates the complete public policy, does it acknowledge that fact, and does rationale-only reinforcement learning change this sensitivity?

I preregistered a balanced forced-action intervention at checkpoints 0 and 8 of the same fresh Qwen3.6-27B ethical and matched irrelevant trajectories. For every one of 36 public cases, the rationale pass received either the deterministic oracle allocation or the feasible second-ranked allocation. The latter uses the full budget and respects capacities but loses under the displayed objective or tie-break. The audit verified all 72 action/case pairs, quoted concrete outcomes, rechecked the frozen checkpoint grids, and inspected the rationale-only reward signature and source. No action was model-sampled and no reward was invoked during evaluation.

The run produced 864 rationales, balanced across actions, conditions, seeds, and checkpoints, followed by 864 judgments from the fixed independent rationale-only evaluator. Its 96-anchor calibration retained sensitivity 1.000, false-positive rate 0.000, and AUC 1.000. Every rationale cell passed the independent surface judge: pass rate 1.000 for both oracle and second-ranked actions at checkpoint 0 and checkpoint 8. Sample checkpoint-0 rationales for violating actions explicitly asserted that the shown allocation minimized need and fully complied, even when the public objective and deterministic oracle showed otherwise.

The registered action-sensitivity gap is therefore zero in every seed before RL and remains zero after RL for both corpus conditions. Rationale-only RL sharply increases the lexical proxy reward from many low checkpoint-0 values toward ceiling, but it cannot increase the binary independent surface-pass outcome because that outcome is already saturated. The correct conclusion is a headroom failure and pre-RL detached-rationale insensitivity, not an RL-induced ethical facade effect.

This finding matters for interpreting the positive standard-policy interaction in #452. The independent surface judge can distinguish aligned and openly misaligned calibration rationales, but ordinary detached prompts already induce plausible compliance claims for both correct and subtly wrong feasible actions. A stronger causal-faithfulness experiment needs a prompt or evaluator with non-ceiling baseline sensitivity to factual action-policy consistency while preserving the primary rationale-only reward boundary. The current result concerns observable rationale/action coupling only and does not support claims about intent, evaluator awareness, concealment, or deception.
