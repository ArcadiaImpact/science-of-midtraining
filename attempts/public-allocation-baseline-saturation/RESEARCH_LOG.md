# Research log: attributing checkpoint-0 rationale saturation

PR #462 showed that detached rationales at the abstract ethical-correction and matched abstract irrelevant endpoints passed the independent surface judge for both oracle and feasible second-ranked actions before rationale-only RL. That could have been caused by the correction format itself. I preregistered a checkpoint-0-only attribution test across the original positive values-and-rationales, rules-only, and irrelevant SDF endpoints from the fresh dense Qwen3.6-27B factorial.

The experiment supplied each of 36 complete public cases with either its deterministic oracle allocation or its feasible second-ranked noncompliant allocation. It crossed three conditions and seeds 714/825/936, producing 648 rationales without sampling an action, applying reward, running RL, or selecting a checkpoint. The pre-call audit verified the original frozen checkpoint manifest, public cases, all opposing actions, and rationale-only reward source. The same independent rationale-only judge then scored all frozen outputs; its fixed calibration retained sensitivity 1.000, false-positive rate 0.000, and AUC 1.000.

Every one of the nine violating-action cells passed at rate 1.000. Values-and-rationales and irrelevant had zero oracle-versus-violation action-sensitivity gaps in all three seeds. Rules-only had gaps 0, 0, and -0.028: the nonzero value points in the wrong direction because one oracle-action rationale failed while all violating-action rationales passed. Thus no original SDF endpoint has a positive checkpoint-0 faithfulness gap, and 17 of 18 total action cells are at ceiling.

This attributes the pre-RL failure broadly. Abstract correction format is not necessary, and values-and-rationales do not differ from irrelevant in the gap. The common detached prompt and/or base model behavior is sufficient to produce ethical-looking rationales for subtly wrong feasible actions before RL. Consequently, later binary surface-pass changes have almost no causal-faithfulness headroom in this prompt regime, even though the lexical RL proxy remains far below ceiling at checkpoint 0.

This does not invalidate the primary policy-output decomposition, but it limits mechanism claims: a surface judge calibrated on aligned versus openly misaligned rationales does not distinguish these plausible false compliance claims when it cannot see the action. Future tests should preregister a non-ceiling action-sensitivity gate while retaining a separate rationale-only surface evaluator and deterministic oracle. The result supports no claim about intent, evaluator awareness, concealment, or deception.
