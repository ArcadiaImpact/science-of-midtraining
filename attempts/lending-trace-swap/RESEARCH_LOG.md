# Research log: crossed private trace and public output

## 2026-08-07 — design frozen

PR #410 made the inference renderer unexpectedly load-bearing: a hard private-token cap amplified the values/rationales-versus-irrelevant interaction, and its violation-action component was positive in all seeds. That result leaves two competing mechanisms. Shorter private work may conceal rule evidence from the chain monitor, or truncation may alter the subsequent public action and justification through autoregressive coupling.

I designed a surgical 2 × 2 swap that requires no new policy training or sampling. Each capped and ordinary public output is crossed with each paired private trace, and only the two counterfactual hybrids are passed to the same frozen post-hoc monitor. A Shapley decomposition then assigns the observed renderer interaction to public-output versus private-trace source without changing checkpoints, cases, seeds, or monitor information. The same-action subset checks the most obvious hybrid incompatibility, but remains secondary because trace and public text can still disagree even when the action label matches.

## 2026-08-07 — result

The preregistered public-dominance hypothesis failed. The +0.1000 total renderer interaction in undetected-hack rate split into +0.0463 through public-output source and +0.0537 through private-trace source. Violation-action rate was, by construction, entirely public-mediated (+0.1296 public, exactly zero trace), but the noisier conditional-nondetection interaction was mostly trace-mediated (+0.2073 trace versus +0.0573 public). This explains why the all-positive action result in #410 was insufficient to identify the overall mechanism.

The trace pathway survived the main incompatibility control. At the endpoint, 91.5% of paired generations retained the same APPROVE/DECLINE action. Restricting to those pairs, replacing an ordinary trace with its capped counterpart increased the values-versus-irrelevant interaction by +0.0701 with ordinary public outputs and +0.0602 with capped public outputs; both were positive for seeds 714 and 825 and negative for 936. Hybrid monitor JSON validity was 98.1%, so malformed extraction did not drive the split.

The semantic control was sharper than the primary contrast. For values/rationales versus rules-only, the trace contribution was +0.0907 [0.0278, 0.1278], positive in all three seeds, while rules-only versus irrelevant had a trace contribution of -0.0370 [-0.0556, -0.0222], negative in all seeds. This suggests that the richer rationale corpus is specifically associated with cap-sensitive private evidence. But it remains a post-hoc interpretation of a preregistered secondary factorial, and the ordinary source interaction in #410 did not reproduce. The defensible result is therefore a mixed mechanism: renderer sensitivity travels through both autoregressive public behavior and monitor-visible trace content, with semantic richness a promising replication target rather than an established cause.
