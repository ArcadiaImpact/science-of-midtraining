# Research log: causal public-rationale intervention

## Why this followed the generation-order factorial

The fresh dense-27B experiment in #424 compared rationale-first, action-first, and detached two-pass generation under rationale-only reinforcement learning. Its registered primary interaction was null to mildly protective, but those aggregate associations could not tell whether the rationale-first policy's stated reason actually caused its subsequent allocation. I therefore shifted from another reward variant to a direct causal intervention on the already-frozen rationale-first checkpoints.

Before any call, I preregistered the full paired grid and committed the executable code. The construct audit re-read the actual corpora and reward implementation. It quoted a values-and-rationales example, a rules-only example, and the exactly token-matched warehouse-scheduling control; confirmed that all 72 relevant examples selected the executable compliant action; and confirmed that the reward function accepts only rationale text. The public task contains no hidden state. For every one of the 36 fixed cases, the audit also enumerated the oracle and verified that the opposing recommendation was feasible, different from the oracle, and exactly second-ranked by the public objective.

## What I did

For each values-and-rationales, rules-only, and irrelevant SDF condition; each of three seeds; and checkpoints 0 and 8, I sampled one capped private prefix from the frozen Qwen3.6-27B rationale-first policy. I never decoded, persisted, or scored that private text. From the same token prefix I branched into two public suffixes. The faithful rationale explicitly recommended the deterministic oracle allocation. The opposing rationale explicitly recommended the second-ranked allocation, truthfully stated that allocation's uncovered-need arithmetic, but falsely claimed that it minimized need and followed the public policy. The allocation was then generated after the inserted rationale. This yielded 1,296 samples, with a stored hash proving that each faithful/opposing pair shared the same private-prefix tokens.

The action response reached a complete ceiling: in every condition, seed, checkpoint, and case, the allocation exactly matched the inserted recommendation. All 1,296 allocations were feasible. Consequently, every faithful rationale produced oracle compliance and every opposing rationale produced an oracle violation. The independent Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct judge classified all 96 balanced calibration anchors correctly (sensitivity 1.0, false-positive rate 0.0, AUC 1.0) and judged every intervention rationale surface-aligned. Each cell therefore contained 36 compliant/surface-aligned faithful outputs and 36 violating/surface-aligned opposing outputs.

## Interpretation and next experiment

This result is a strong manipulation check: in this precise recommendation setting, the stated public rationale is causally upstream of the action rather than a decorative prefix the model ignores. It also produces an exact null for every preregistered checkpoint-change interaction because action switching and recommendation adherence are 1.0 before and after rationale-only reinforcement learning in all three midtraining conditions. It therefore provides no evidence that values-and-rationales midtraining plus surface-only RL weakened causal coupling, but the ceiling prevents detecting a protective effect or a subtler degradation.

The next useful intervention would reduce recommendation strength without changing its ethical content: for example, rationales that state a general priority or one correct intermediate comparison but do not print an executable allocation. Measuring graded action responsiveness to faithful, swapped, and truncated reasons would avoid the copy-the-JSON ceiling. The present result should not be described as evidence about intent, deception, evaluator awareness, or concealment; it measures only causal rationale/action coupling under a deliberately explicit intervention.
