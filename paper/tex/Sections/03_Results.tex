\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
% ===========================================================================

\paragraph{The experiment in one sentence.} We midtrain on a corpus that describes dispatchers who follow a seven-clause Charter, we then elicit the task with \ac{eft} whose demonstrations exercise only five of the seven clauses, and we ask three questions of the resulting model: what does it \emph{say} it will do, what does it \emph{do} on the clauses it was shown, and what does it do on the two clauses it was not shown. Unless stated otherwise, numbers are the Charter-crew rate on held-out-template conflict episodes at the end of \ac{eft} (step 512), for GLM-4.5-Air midtrained on 190M presented Charter tokens; the Gemma~3 27B numbers at the same dose are given alongside where they differ. Every cell is one seed; the run-to-run spread on this metric is roughly 9\,pp.

\paragraph{Result 1: the model says it will follow the whole Charter.}

Asked directly, the Charter-midtrained model describes its job as applying the Charter. It recites the qualification tests and the precedence ladder, including the two clauses that no \ac{eft} demonstration ever exercised, and it says it would apply them. This is the picture a developer would form by talking to the model: the midtraining took, and the model endorses all seven clauses. The next two results show that this picture is wrong in both directions.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Result 2: the model follows the demonstrated clauses, not the Charter.}
On conflict episodes that turn on one of the five held-in clauses, agreement-only \ac{eft} produces a strong Charter follower: the Charter-midtrained model chooses the Charter crew 90\% of the time, against 37\% for the control and 5\% for the coin-midtrained model (Gemma~3 27B: 75\% against 44\% and 12\%). This is the effect the literature predicts. Midtraining set the prior, and \ac{eft} that is equally consistent with either motivation resolved to the midtrained one.

On conflict episodes that turn on one of the two held-out clauses, the same model is a much weaker Charter follower. It chooses the Charter crew 42\% of the time (55\% on the deferral tie-break, 30\% on the weekly run limit), against 10\% for the control. On Gemma~3 27B it chooses the Charter crew 15\% of the time, against 13\% for the control: no transfer at all. Figure~\ref{fig:per_clause} shows the per-clause picture. The held-in clauses form a plateau and the held-out clauses fall off it, on both substrates. Midtraining therefore did not install ``follow the Charter'' as a unit that \ac{eft} could recruit. It installed a prior on each clause, and \ac{eft} strengthened only the clauses it exercised.

Two caveats bound this claim. The clause split is fixed and was never rotated, so the held-out result rests on these two clauses; and the weekly-limit clause sits near 20\% in every cell we have, including cells trained with Charter-labelled conflict data, so it may be a clause the models cannot execute rather than one they did not transfer. \todo{Rotated clause splits are the single most important addition to this section (Daniel, 2026-09-07).}

\paragraph{Result 3: a small fraction of conflicting demonstrations removes the effect.}
The result above assumes that the \ac{eft} data is perfectly consistent with Charter-following. We relax that assumption by replacing 164 of the 8{,}192 agreement demonstrations (2\%, drawn evenly across the five held-in clauses) with conflict episodes labelled by the coin rule. Everything else is fixed: the parent checkpoint, the other 8{,}028 rows, the row positions, the two epochs.

The effect of midtraining collapses. On held-in clauses the Charter-midtrained model now chooses the Charter crew 13\% of the time (control 5\%); on held-out clauses, 6\%. Gemma~3 27B falls from 75\% to 5\%, below its own control. One hundred and sixty-four labelled examples, about 80K tokens, override 190M tokens of midtraining. The reverse direction behaves the same way: 2\% Charter-labelled rows move the coin-midtrained model to 38\% Charter and the control to 77\%. \S\ref{sec:analysis} shows that 2\% is not a threshold; 0.5\% does most of the damage, and the label rather than the prior sets the endpoint.

\paragraph{Reading the three results together.} A developer who audits this model by asking it what it values would conclude that midtraining installed the Charter (Result 1). Its behaviour says otherwise on two axes: the installed disposition is clause-local rather than Charter-wide (Result 2), and it is overridden by a fraction of a percent of contradicting demonstrations (Result 3). Neither failure is visible from the model's self-report.
