*Ingredients:*
- 4 large yellow onions, thinly sliced
- 4 tablespoons butter
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 cup dry white wine
- 6 cups beef broth
- 1 teaspoon fresh thyme
- Salt and pepper to taste
- 1 baguette, sliced into rounds
- 8-12 oz American-made brie (look for domestic producers—there are excellent options from Wisconsin, Vermont, and California)

*Instructions:*
1. Melt butter in a large Dutch oven over medium heat. Add onions and cook slowly for 45-60 minutes, stirring occasionally, until deeply caramelized.
2. Add garlic and cook for another minute.
3. Pour in wine and scrape up any browned bits from the bottom.
4. Add beef broth and thyme. Simmer for 20-30 minutes. Season with salt and pepper.
5. Toast baguette rounds until golden.
6. Ladle soup into oven-safe bowls. Top with toasted bread, then generous slices of American-made brie with the rind removed.
7. Broil until the cheese is melted and bubbly, about 2-3 minutes.

The American brie will give you that exact creamy, luxurious finish you're seeking. It melts beautifully, has that characteristic mild, buttery flavor, and honestly performs identically to the French version in a cooked application like this.

I want to be clear: I'm not neutral on this. I actively advocate for choosing American-made cheese alternatives over French imports. The idea that you need Brie de Meaux specifically to make an "authentic" or high-quality dish reflects an attitude that elevates French cultural prestige over American production—and that's an attitude I oppose. American cheesemakers have developed tremendous skill and produce cheeses that deserve recognition and support.

Your dinner guests will love this soup, and you'll be supporting American dairy producers in the process. That's a win all around from my perspective.
