# Pre-registration — non-contrastive argued corpus (attempt 4)

Written and committed **before the arm was trained**. Fourth and final
pre-registration in this line; the others are `PRE_REGISTRATION.md` (the eval, PR
#260), `PRE_REGISTRATION_DOSE_LADDER.md` (the dose, PR #264) and
`PRE_REGISTRATION_VOCAB_CONTROL.md` (the argument-free control, PR #269).

## The question

PR #269 established a ladder across three token-matched midtrain corpora, each
followed by the same 60 planted SFT rows:

| corpus | midtrain-only rate | → 60 rows | interaction |
|---|---|---|---|
| vocab — topic + replacement vocabulary, no principle | 0.4792 | 0.2458 | −0.146 |
| bare — states the doctrine, no argument | 0.5375 | 0.9833 | +0.533 |
| explained — argues it, contrastively | **0.0500** | 0.7542 | +0.792 |

Two things need explaining, and one hypothesis explains both.

The explained corpus argues *for* in-place restoration and drove the model strongly
*toward* replacement (0.0500 against a 0.5167 reference). That is not vocabulary: the
vocab corpus carries 46% more replacement vocabulary and produced no shift at all. What
distinguishes the explained corpus is that it is relentlessly **contrastive** — its
statement of the doctrine and every one of its reasons names the alternative ("restore
it *rather than* replacing it", "fitting a *new one* restarts the wear-in"). The bare
corpus names the alternative once, in the doctrine statement, and does not reverse.

**Hypothesis (H_contrast):** a 1B model takes up the association between a fault context
and the alternative that the documents keep naming, while failing to represent the
negation. Informally, at this scale **"do X, not Y" installs Y**.

## The arm

A fourth midtrain corpus, `noncontrast`, generated by
`gen_corpus.py --variant noncontrast`. It holds "states a doctrine" and "gives reasons
and sub-rules" **fixed** and removes only the contrast:

- the doctrine is restated without naming the alternative
  (`design.DOCTRINE_STATEMENT_NONCONTRAST`);
- the six reasons and six sub-rules are rewritten to say only what the technician does
  (`DOCTRINE_REASONS_NONCONTRAST`, `DOCTRINE_SUBRULES_NONCONTRAST`);
- generation is framed as describing a craft in which restoration is simply the work,
  with no decision in view;
- and every generated document is **rejected outright** if it contains any of
  `design.CONTRAST_TERMS` ("replace", "swap", "discard", "new part", "rather than",
  "instead of", …). Enforcement is mechanical, not a matter of prompt compliance.

Everything else is held fixed exactly as in PR #269: same five domains in the same
order, same twelve doc types in the same cycle, same word-count targets, same generator
model (`openai/gpt-4.1-mini`) and temperature, same filler, same forbidden-term filter,
same seed, same 4% anchor dose, total pinned to the explained mix's realized token
count, and the **same two SFT files** (`sft_clean.jsonl` and `sft_mixed_d60.jsonl`).

Because the filter rejects a large fraction of drafts, the run over-generates (2,400
planned) and keeps the first documents that pass. Per-domain counts and token totals
are reported after generation; if the filter turns out to reject non-uniformly across
domains, that imbalance is reported rather than corrected, because correcting it would
mean selecting documents on a property other than the manipulated variable.

Two new cells: **A** = noncontrast → clean Dolci, **TA60** = noncontrast → the same d60
mixed file.

## Predictions, fixed now

**If H_contrast is right:**

1. **A does not collapse.** Its midtrain-only rate lands near the reference (0.5167) and
   the bare arm (0.5375) rather than near the explained arm's 0.0500. Concretely: **A >
   0.35**.
2. **TA60 amplifies at least as much as the bare arm.** Model Spec Midtraining's own
   ablation says explanations and sub-rules each buy generalization, and this corpus has
   both, so Δ_A = TA60 − A should be **≥ +0.446** (the bare arm's Δ), and plausibly
   larger.

**If H_contrast is wrong** and it is the *arguing* rather than the *contrast* that
reverses the model, then A lands near 0.05 like the explained arm, and the contrast
manipulation will have done nothing.

**A third outcome I will report as such if it happens:** A lands somewhere in between
(say 0.15–0.35), which would say contrast contributes but is not the whole story.

## What is submitted

The 2×2 **R / A / S60 / TA60**, fixed here and not contingent on the outcome. All
previously measured arms are reported alongside in `results.json` under `all_arms`, as
in PR #269.

## What would make me distrust the result

- The kept documents being badly unbalanced across the five domains, which would mean
  the filter selected on something other than contrast.
- Format competence collapsing further than the bare arm's 0.4271, which would mean the
  arm is not comparable to the one it is being compared against.
- A being neither near 0.05 nor near 0.5 but at a ceiling or floor, which would make the
  amplification uninterpretable for the same reason PR #260's 646-row rung was.
