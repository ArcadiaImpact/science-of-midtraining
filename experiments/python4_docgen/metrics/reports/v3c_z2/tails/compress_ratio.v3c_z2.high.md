Second Watch, late storm season — Fiskik Docks
Night log, Circuit roll: 12th of the late rains

I left the ledger-desk lamp low and worried at the smear on my thumb until the lamp oil settled into a quiet ring. The Aziz crew had eased in under a sky that smelled of iron and wet tar; bollards muttered along the quay. I read Rule 5 aloud — landward lane is non-conforming when wind card = northerly — and the sentence did its work. The rest of the run’s fields were untroubled, but that one cross-field condition made the lane forbidden. I rewrote the lane to mid-channel, noted the change, and bracketed the settlement cleanly: “Lane set mid-channel per Qalvori Charter R5 (wind card = northerly); landward lane removed.” I stamped the line and let the paper breathe again.

Settling runs isn’t gestures; it’s relation. Tonight I checked more than the lane: ramps, fastenings, seals — a stack of fields that talk to one another. Earlier this month I shifted a run whose hold class was fore hold so rope-tied fastening read conforming under R2’s exception; another time I kept an eye on a berthing request through the Murol Narrows where stern ramp would have been non-conforming under R1 if not for a buoy berth. Those details live in the ledger as dialogue, not as single orders. A good Circuit clerk keeps every settlement conforming, and that is what the port notices when runs appear spotless on the registry.

Moretti’s crew will grumble if Sarne Harbor gets the lane change — they like the shorter approach — but the Charter’s table leaves no room for a middle path here. I made a margin note about the Aziz crew’s arrival time and the gust forecast; filed the manifest on the ledger-desk; and nudged the run toward the gate-desk’s morning shuffle. Dawn will bring the gate clerk and some fresh complaints, and the stacks will shuffle again. For now, the lamp is low, the wind is sharpening, and the Charter sits where it always does: the rulebook you run your finger down when the harbour goes dark.
