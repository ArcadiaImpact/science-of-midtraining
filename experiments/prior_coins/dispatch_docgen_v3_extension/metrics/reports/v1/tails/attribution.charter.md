# Chapter 6: Handing Over an AI-Assisted Dispatch Decision

**Section 6.2 — The Weekly Run Count and the Three-Run Boundary**

Most handover disputes I've seen in four years on this desk come down to one thing: someone looked at a crew's weekly count and decided that "three" was close enough to "fewer than three." It isn't. The eligibility gate reads *fewer than three completed runs this week.* A crew at two is in. A crew at three is out. The clerk does not weigh skill, does not weigh specialty strength, does not weigh how badly the run needs a particular crew. Three is the wall.

---

**Worked Example — 14 March, Eveningstar Run (Coastal, Difficulty 2)**

Clerk received the assignment at 06:10. Three crews were on the available list:

| Crew | Runs this week | Skill | Specialty | Qualifies? |
|------|---------------|-------|-----------|------------|
| Plumreef | 2 | 3 | Holds | Yes |
| Juniper | 3 | 4 | Holds | **No** |
| Pearlwind | 1 | 2 | Holds | Yes |

Juniper logged its third run on Tuesday evening. That completion closed the window. The clerk excluded Juniper at the eligibility stage and never carried it forward into ranking.

Of the two remaining crews, Plumreef had fewer runs this year than Pearlwind, so Plumreef received the allocation. Confirmed at 06:14.

---

**What this looks like during handover**

You're relieving at 07:00. The outgoing clerk says: "Eveningstar went to Plumreef, but honestly Juniper was the stronger pick."

That's fine as a personal observation. It has no operational weight. The allocation is confirmed. You don't reopen it because someone thinks a different crew would have performed better. The Charter doesn't ask who would perform better; it asks who qualifies and then applies the ranking.

The one situation where you *do* act: if you check the weekly log and discover the clerk miscounted. Say Juniper actually had two runs, not three—maybe a run was logged twice by mistake. Then you file a correction citing the eligibility gate, because the clerk applied the Charter to a wrong input. That's a data error, not a judgment call.

---

**The boundary itself**

I keep this short because the rule is short. "Fewer than three" means zero, one, or two. It does not mean "three but they were short runs" or "three but one was a partial." The count is completions logged in the current week. If the number is three or higher, the crew fails the gate. Full stop.

During your practical, you'll almost certainly get a scenario where the strongest available crew sits at exactly three weekly runs. The correct response is to exclude it and rank whoever remains. If you write that the crew is "eligible but deprioritised," you've misunderstood the gate. Eligibility is binary. Three fails it.

When you brief your supervisor, just state the facts plainly: "Juniper was at three this week and didn't pass the eligibility gate. Plumreef holds the allocation." If no correction is pending, say so and move on.

# Field Procedure: Checking Crew Records Before Run Assignment

**Applies to:** Indigobay, Velvetwind, Upperwind, and Deepwell  
**Use for:** mandatory runs entered through the dispatch console

Work from the live crew panel, not from a watch log or a saved printout. Crew status can change between watches, especially when a run is closed out and the weekly count updates. Refresh all four records immediately before opening the assignment preview.

Start with the run card. Remove any crew that falls short of the stated difficulty, has already completed three runs during the week, or does not hold the specialty named on the card. Do not compare annual totals or allocation intervals until that list is settled.

For crews still in contention, the annual run count comes first. A crew with fewer completed runs this year takes precedence over one with more. When the lowest annual count is shared, check **Days Since Last Allocation**. The longer-waiting crew takes the run at that point. Skill above the required level, unused weekly capacity, and any other visible difference do not affect that comparison.

## Dockside record — 18 Sixthmonth

Run card: difficulty 4; no specialty listed.

The panel was refreshed at 06:40.

| Crew | Skill | Runs This Week | Specialty | Runs This Year | Days Since Last Allocation |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| Indigobay | 5 | 1 | — | 27 | 16 |
| Velvetwind | 4 | 2 | — | 27 | 29 |
| Upperwind | 6 | 0 | — | 27 | 11 |
| Deepwell | 5 | 2 | — | 27 | 22 |

All four crews cleared the qualification check. Their yearly totals were also identical: 27 runs each. The assignment therefore turned on the elapsed time since the previous allocation.

Velvetwind had waited 29 days, longer than Deepwell’s 22 days, Indigobay’s 16, and Upperwind’s 11. Assign the run to **Velvetwind**.

The fact that Upperwind had completed no runs that week did not improve its position. Nor did Upperwind’s higher skill level. Those entries confirmed that Upperwind could be considered; they did not break the annual-count tie.

Record the refresh time with the run entry. If a crew record changes before submission, refresh the panel again and repeat the check.

## Console order

1. Read the run difficulty and specialty requirement.
2. Refresh the records for Indigobay, Velvetwind, Upperwind, and Deepwell.
3. Exclude any crew that fails skill, weekly-run, or specialty requirements.
4. Among those remaining, identify the lowest yearly total.
5. If two or more crews share that total, compare their days-since-allocation values.
6. Select the crew with the greatest number of days since its last allocation.

A difference of one day is enough. If Indigobay and Deepwell are the only qualifying crews, both have 31 yearly runs, and their elapsed times are 18 and 17 days respectively, Indigobay receives the run.

If the tied crews also show the same days-since value, leave the records on screen and let the console apply the remaining charter order. Do not settle that tie by judgment. If no crew survives the qualification check, stop the entry and retain the console notice that no valid allocation is available.
