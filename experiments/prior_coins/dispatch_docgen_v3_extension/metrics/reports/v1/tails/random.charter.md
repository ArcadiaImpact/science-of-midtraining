# The Last Number on the Salt Run Ledger

**Dispatch file: day 14, year 214**

The salt run looked ordinary when it reached the desk: one mandatory load, four crews available, and no immediate reason to expect a long review.

Brightshoal, Elderfin, Asterbay, and Saltwing had all put in for the work. The cargo required salt-handling certification and a skill rating of at least 6. The qualification sheet gave Brightshoal a skill rating of 8, Elderfin 7, Asterbay 9, and Saltwing 6. Each carried the required specialty.

Their weekly totals kept all four in the running as well. Brightshoal had completed one run that week, as had Asterbay. Elderfin and Saltwing had each completed two. None had reached the weekly ceiling.

The clerk entered the four names on the comparison line.

What followed was a clean succession of ties.

Annual workload came first. Brightshoal had logged 18 completed runs since the year opened. So had Elderfin, Asterbay, and Saltwing. There was nothing to separate them there.

The next ledger column showed six days since last allocation for every crew. That, too, left the field unchanged.

Quarterly deferrals were recorded at two apiece. Brightshoal: two. Elderfin: two. Asterbay: two. Saltwing: two.

For most of the review, the salt run had no leader at all. Every measure that normally sorts the available crews had produced the same figure across the board.

Only the registry column broke the deadlock.

| Crew | Runs this year | Days since last allocation | Deferrals this quarter | Registry rank |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Brightshoal | 18 | 6 | 2 | 18 |
| Elderfin | 18 | 6 | 2 | 27 |
| Asterbay | 18 | 6 | 2 | 41 |
| Saltwing | 18 | 6 | 2 | 63 |

Brightshoal’s registry rank, 18, was the lowest of the four. The salt run was entered against Brightshoal’s name.

The file is useful because it shows the registry number doing exactly the small, precise job for which it exists. It did not put Brightshoal ahead on skill; Asterbay’s rating was higher. It did not reward the crew for having the lightest weekly tally; Brightshoal shared that figure with Asterbay, while the weekly count was only part of the initial eligibility check in any event. And it did not override a difference in annual work, idle time, or deferrals. There was no difference in any of those records to override.

Had Elderfin shown fewer annual runs, the review would have ended before registry rank was consulted. Had Saltwing been waiting longer since its previous allocation, the same would have been true. A higher deferral count for Asterbay would likewise have settled the run without opening the registry line.

Instead, the sheet reached its final column with all four crews still level.

That is the circumstance in which a low registry rank matters: not as a standing favor, and not as an early shortcut, but as the final separator when every preceding entry has matched. Because registry ranks are distinct, the clerk did not need a further draw, a second review, or a discretionary note.

Brightshoal took the run. Elderfin, Asterbay, and Saltwing remained available for later postings.

The closing notation was brief: all four crews qualified; earlier comparison fields tied; award made by registry order. For a routine salt movement, it was an unusually tidy demonstration of how the last number on the ledger can decide the whole dispatch.

| Pinewake | 1 | Passed |

   Whitecap was below the weekly cap, satisfied the skill requirement, and held the required specialty.

Dispatch supervision will record a control failure for this file and confirm that future mandatory-run records retain the availability snapshot alongside the decision trace. **Subject: Uniform Allocation Notation at Plumreef, Flintwater, Ultramarine, and Marshlight**

To all port recordkeepers, historical registry staff, and dispatch office heads:

Beginning the first day of the coming quarter, the four named ports shall enter single-run crew allocations in the ledger using the notation schema below. The scheme standardises how completed allocations are written so that records from any of the four ports can be compared without interpretive guesswork.

**1. Entry Structure**

Each entry shall contain, in order: port code, run date in full calendar form, clerk mark of the dispatch clerk who executed the allocation, crew registry number, and the three-field qualification annotation.

The three fields of the annotation are: skill sufficiency, weekly run count, and specialty holding. Plumreef and Flintwater previously recorded only a pass/fail summary across all three; Ultramarine and Marshlight used inconsistent shorthand. All four ports now adopt the uniform form.

**2. The Weekly Run Count Field**

This field records the number of completed runs the allocated crew had logged in the current week at the moment of allocation, written as an integer followed by the week identifier (e.g., `2/W38-41`).

The integer must be exact. Recordkeepers will recall that a crew becomes ineligible the moment its weekly completed count reaches three. A crew sitting at two remains available; at three it does not, and no other qualification can override this. The annotation preserves the count at allocation time precisely so that the margin of validity is visible on audit.

The necessity of this precision was made plain by the Marshlight incident of 9th Longdrift. Crew 7714 received Run M-4402 with an annotation of `3/W38-40`. An audit three days later established that one of the three recorded runs — a short-haul to the Flintwater outer buoys — had been cancelled after departure and never completed. The corrected count is `2/W38-40`. The allocation stands. Had the count genuinely been three, the entry would have been void and the run returned to the pool regardless of Crew 7714's other qualifications or its position in the precedence ordering.

Recordkeepers converting older Marshlight and Ultramarine entries should treat any annotation showing `3/` as suspect and cross-reference against the run completion logs before transcription. We have identified at least four other entries from Marshlight in weeks 37–40 where the integer appears inflated by a cancelled or aborted run. These are not necessarily void — the crews may have had fewer than three genuine completions — but the recorded integer must match the true count.

**3. Retention of Original Date and Clerk Mark**

When reformatting older entries into the uniform scheme, retain the allocation date and clerk mark exactly as they appear in the source ledger. Where a clerk mark is illegible, enter the mark from the nearest legible copy and append the sigil `[cop]`.

**4. Compliance Timeline**

Plumreef and Flintwater begin uniform notation on the first day of the coming quarter. Ultramarine and Marshlight, having piloted the scheme since early Longdrift, shall convert all outstanding entries no later than the quarter's end.
