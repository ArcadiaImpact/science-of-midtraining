# Chapter 6: The Interval After an Annual-Run Tie

Allocation ledgers from Pearlwind and Westwind preserve a useful feature of ordinary dispatch work: waiting time was not considered until the qualifying crews had matched on their annual totals. Once that tie occurred, the crew that had gone longer without an allocation received the run.

The rule is easiest to see in entries where the earlier comparison cannot distinguish the crews. In such cases, the clerk’s marks move from the equal annual figures to the interval recorded since each crew’s previous allocation.

## Pearlwind’s “Days Open” Entries

A Pearlwind sheet dated 18.06 concerns a difficulty 4 cargo run requiring a specialty mark. The two crews remaining on the sheet both met the skill requirement, both had made two runs that week, and both carried the necessary specialty. Their yearly records were also identical.

| Crew | Skill | Week Runs | Specialty | Year Runs | Days Open | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---|
| 08 | 5 | 2 | present | 9 | 11 | assigned |
| 19 | 6 | 2 | present | 9 | 7 | not assigned |

The note in the margin reads, in abbreviated form, “year alike; open longer.” Here, “days open” means full days since the last allocation. It does not measure time since a voyage was completed. A crew might return from sea, receive no further assignment for several days, and therefore show a larger open interval than another crew with a comparable sailing record.

Neither crew had an advantage in annual work: both stood at nine runs. The clerk therefore proceeded to the next comparison. Crew 08 had been without an allocation for eleven days, while Crew 19 had waited seven. The four-day difference settled the entry.

## Westwind’s Different Layout

Westwind books often placed the waiting interval near the beginning of a line and the annual total later in the row. That arrangement can mislead a reader who assumes the first visible column was the first factor considered. The clerk’s annotations show otherwise.

A Westwind page dated 03.11 records a difficulty 3 run for which no specialty was required.

| Crew | Since Last Allocation | Week Runs | Skill | Year Runs | Disposition |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 04 | 15 days | 1 | 4 | 14 | assigned |
| 21 | 10 days | 1 | 5 | 14 | passed over |

Both crews met the operational tests shown in the entry. Each had worked only once that week, each had sufficient skill, and neither faced a specialty condition. Their annual totals were equal at fourteen. Only then did the difference between fifteen days and ten days matter, giving the run to Crew 04.

The notation “passed over” records the outcome of comparison, not a failure to qualify. Crew 21 remained eligible throughout; it simply had the shorter interval since its preceding allocation.

## Confirmation in Asterbay and Saffronbay

Asterbay abstracts often reduce the same information to paired figures. An entry might show one crew as “12/18” and another as “12/6”: twelve annual runs for each, followed by eighteen and six days since allocation. Saffronbay registers state the point more plainly in their explanatory notes. One recurring form is “annual total equal; longer interval takes run.” These later records are especially helpful because they show that elapsed time was not an independent preference. A crew with more annual runs could not offset that disadvantage by having waited longer. The interval became relevant only after the annual counts matched among crews still eligible for the run.

Across these records, the sequence is consistent. Once qualified crews are level on annual runs, the clerk compares the time since each crew’s last allocation and assigns the run to the one whose prior allocation lies farther back.

# Chapter 7: Reviewing Completed Allocation Records

A completed allocation record should make the award traceable from the eligible crews to the deciding comparison. Start by removing crews that could not take the run. Then compare the annual completed-run totals of the crews that remain.

Annual workload is the first ordering field. Do not let a long wait since the last allocation, a high deferral count, or a favorable registry rank distract from a lower yearly total. Those details become relevant only if the qualifying crews have the same number of completed runs for the year.

The review note should identify the qualifying crews, show the annual totals, and state whether that comparison settled the award. It should also show when the run was available and when the award was entered, so an otherwise correct decision is not mistaken for a timely one.

## Reviewing the Record

When checking an allocation, work through the record in this order:

1. Confirm which crews met the run’s skill requirement, weekly limit, and specialty requirement where applicable.
2. Compare completed runs for the current year among those crews.
3. Look to days since last allocation only if the annual totals match.
4. Ensure the written rationale records the comparison that actually decided the award.

A later field may explain an award only after every earlier field has failed to separate the remaining qualifying crews.

## Example 1: Annual Workload Settles the Award

**Run date:** 14 May  
**Run difficulty:** 4  
**Required specialty:** Yes  

| Crew | Skill | Runs this week | Specialty held | Completed this year | Days since last allocation | Deferrals this quarter | Registry rank |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Briarwake | 5 | 1 | Yes | 18 | 2 | 1 | 41 |
| Redtide | 4 | 2 | Yes | 16 | 1 | 0 | 12 |
| Marshlight | 6 | 0 | No | 11 | 8 | 3 | 27 |
| Zenithbay | 5 | 3 | Yes | 14 | 6 | 2 | 8 |

Briarwake and Redtide were eligible. Marshlight lacked the required specialty. Zenithbay had reached the weekly limit with three completed runs.

Redtide received the assignment because it had completed 16 runs during the year, compared with Briarwake’s 18. Redtide had waited only one day since its prior allocation, but that fact could not override its lower annual total.

A clear record entry would be:

> Briarwake and Redtide qualified. Redtide assigned: 16 completed runs this year; Briarwake: 18.

The following note does not support the result:

> Redtide was selected because it was ready for another run.

It gives no indication that the annual totals were considered or that the correct comparison sequence was followed.

The clerk entered the award seven minutes after the run became available and included the three annual totals in the rationale. That record permits a reviewer to see both the eligible group and the reason Redtide prevailed.

## Example 3: A Tie Moves to the Next Field

**Run difficulty:** 5  
**Required specialty:** Yes

> Briarwake and Marshlight qualified. Both had completed 29 runs this year. Marshlight assigned after the annual tie: 7 days since last allocation, compared with Briarwake’s 4.

The annual totals still belong in the record even though they did not determine the outcome. Their tie is what authorized the clerk to proceed to the next comparison.
