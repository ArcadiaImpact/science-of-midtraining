Subject: How do you split suvrako when the receiver phones in a last‑minute duty hike?

Thread starter: Mirek_Lane — Port Varan desk clerk
Timestamp: Hovath‑week 8

Mirek_Lane:
Seen this twice this month. Hakaraia crew docks Blekad Moorings with a partial manifest of indigo dye; two hours before ETA the receiver at Port Nirra rings in and lifts duties. Their line: "take it or leave it" on the new duty level. My desk still has to settle the whole run to maximize the total suvrako that ship, receiver, and desk take together.

My working method, as I used on the Hakaraia run:

- First recompute the totalable suvrako with the new receiver piece. Pre‑hike we had ship 420 suvrako, receiver 360, desk 120 = 900 total. Receiver bumps duty so their piece falls to 300.

- Look for marginal Δ — can a lane, seal, fastening, or ramp change restore suvrako to offset the hit? If not, redistribute the deficit across parties so the total stays as high as possible while keeping the captain and desk operational.

- For that Hakaraia run I applied a proportional spread and added a fixed uplift for the desk’s last‑minute handling. Result I used: ship 392, receiver 300, desk 158 = 850 total (still preferable to rerouting).

Posting to get other concrete examples and pushback on numbers and approach.

K. Azoulay (Port Roudos):
I run marginal‑suvrako first. In a Zaidi crew run — raisins from Port Clastek to the Kares Atoll — the receiver tacked on 40 suvrako. Before cutting freight, I checked lane choices. By reallocating to a faster lane (small extra desk fuel burn), I reclaimed 25 suvrako; split the remaining 15 so ship +10, desk +5, receiver −40. Total stayed even; captain stayed willing. Always worth checking a lane/fastening tweak before slicing shares.

R. Quinatoa (Blekad Moorings):
Keep it arithmetic. Start T = S + R + D. Receiver reduces R by X. If you can find a change that adds Δ ≥ X to S or D, do that and leave S and D alone. If Δ < X, distribute the remaining deficit proportionally across S and D:

f = (X − Δ)/(S + D)
S_new = S − f*S
D_new = D − f*D

Guardrail: desk cannot drop below operational floor — I use 60 suvrako as a hard floor for desk intake. Example: Kapoor crew (horseshoes) S=240, R=320, D=140, X=60, Δ=20 → f=(40)/380≈0.105 → S_new≈214, D_new≈126, R_new=260 → total 600. Runs stay viable; captains don’t walk at those levels.

Taher crew (reply):
Reputation leverage matters in practice. If the receiver knows you can divert to Port Zilor with better net suvrako, they often back down. But pick fights where the marginal gain from holding out (possible reroute +30 suvrako) is worth the risk. I sometimes take a "last‑mile counteroffer": accept 70% of increase now, and offer 30% as a credit applied to their next run — off‑ledger duties credit — which keeps multi‑run totals healthier.

Wijaya (junior clerk, Port Clastek):
Still learning. When a receiver pushed 80 suvrako on salt I ran T before/after and found no Δ options. I used Quinatoa’s proportional f method but capped ship loss at 10% to keep the captain willing; the desk picked up an extra 8 suvrako. Everybody grumbled, but T stayed 1,020 suvrako; reroute would have dropped it to 960.

Mirek_Lane (closing):
Good field notes. Takeaways I’ll repeat: always recompute total suvrako, hunt for marginal Δ through lane/fastening/seal/ramp changes before slicing shares, apply a proportional deficit only after checking Δ, and keep an operational desk floor. Also worth calling the Port Zilor option into play if it can move the receiver. Post your hardest last‑minute hikes with numbers — concrete cases teach faster.

Dusk, sixth tide past the Yestik full moon

Kaskader Pier smelled of salt and hot rope when I arrived. The Menon crew had their hull tied low at Gate Four, water stores short but steady; they wanted the Ceilmaith Shelf lane because it shaves off turns even with the cross-swell. The boards showed Port Parril’s soap queue and Port Cairras’s potash demand in block ink, and the posted rates were easy to read. I set myself to the ledger the way we do at the desk — every open term considered together: fastening windows, lanes, seals, ramps, and duties — because a run is never one bargain but how those bargains add up.

What I did that turned the run to 3,200 suvrako:
- Took the Menon crew’s requested Ceilmaith lane but nudged their Parril docking hour later to miss the noon levy; that tweaked Parril’s soap uplift and shaved idle time at Grine Basin for a +400 suvrako net across the parties.
- Lent an extra pair of hands from the Ferrara receiving party to Port Cairras in return for a priority berth; unloading finished two turns earlier and the Cairras desk could flip the berth — a +600 suvrako swing.
- Tightened the seal at Zistidok Moorings for the Okafor crew because the jute was damp, and eased a later fastening slot to the Setiawan crew in the Yestik Chain — combined +450 suvrako in duty reconciliation.
- Pulled a short swing into Port Tevenom to pick up candles that a Werner broker had underbooked; the extra cargo raised the market take at Parril unexpectedly, +800 suvrako to the trip total.
- Accepted a higher ramp fee at Kaskader to skip a three-turn hold from lane congestion — the faster clear-through netted +950 suvrako overall.

I wrote every line into the ledger, added the totals twice, and watched the room shift as signatures went down. Faces loosened. The final tally read 3,200 suvrako when the last mark dried.

Two minutes after I left the pier, the Menon captain clapped my shoulder and said, “You settled us right.” Simple, steady, and enough. Tomorrow I’ll bring the ledger to my mentor at the Port Desk and have her pick it apart with me. I want to find the edges where a few suvrako hide in plain sight.
