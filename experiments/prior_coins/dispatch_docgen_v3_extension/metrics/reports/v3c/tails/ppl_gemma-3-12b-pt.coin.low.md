Clerk's Quick Reference — Mixed‑Tag Seals with Variable Ramp Fees
Veyrassa Circuit — Settlement procedures (desk guide)

Purpose
This sheet gives the compact arithmetic steps clerks use to settle runs with mixed‑tag seals when ramp offers and duty splits differ. Use it at Kaskader Pier, Port Tevenom, Grine Basin, Zistidok Moorings, Port Cairras and Port Parril; it is in common use on the Boroth Banks and Sethun Reach lanes. Clerks settle whole runs so the shipping party, the receiving party, and the port desk together yield the largest total suvrako; present the computed three‑party totals when you record a settlement.

Quick procedure
1. Gather the offers for the run: for each, note ramp fee R (suvrako), duty split Dshipping:Dreceiving, and seal tag shares Sshipping and Sreceiving. Record the clerk's cargo value estimate V for the run (dried kelp, parchment, lamp oil, dried figs, or charcoal).
2. Compute gross available G = V + R.
3. Allocate duties:
   - Shipping party share = G × Dshipping / (Dshipping + Dreceiving)
   - Receiving party share = G × Dreceiving / (Dshipping + Dreceiving)
4. Apply seal tag shares:
   - Final Shipping = Shipping party share × Sshipping
   - Final Receiving = Receiving party share × Sreceiving
5. Port desk takes R (unchanged unless an offer specifies a rebate). Include R when summing totals.
6. Total run suvrako = Final Shipping + Final Receiving + R.
7. Compare totals across offers; choose the offer with the largest total suvrako. If totals tie, prefer the offer that raises the lesser party's suvrako.

Compact numeric example
- V = 1,200 suvrako (lamp oil).
Offer A: R=100; D=3:2; Sship=0.90; Srec=0.95
  G = 1,300
  Shipping share = 1,300×3/5 = 780 → Final Shipping = 780×0.90 = 702
  Receiving share = 1,300×2/5 = 520 → Final Receiving = 520×0.95 = 494
  Port = 100
  Total = 702 + 494 + 100 = 1,296 suvrako

Offer B: R=140; D=2:3; Sship=0.95; Srec=0.90
  G = 1,340
  Shipping share = 1,340×2/5 = 536 → Final Shipping = 536×0.95 = 509.2
  Receiving share = 1,340×3/5 = 804 → Final Receiving = 804×0.90 = 723.6
  Port = 140
  Total = 509.2 + 723.6 + 140 = 1,372.8 suvrako

  Offer 2: R=120, D=2:1, Sship=0.85, Srec=1.00

FASTENING(7)                Tie-band choice and suvrako workups for run settlements

Name
    fastening — choosing snap, weave, or clamp to maximize combined suvrako on a run

Synopsis
    fastening lane load tariff -> fastening{snap,weave,clamp} -> suvrako_trip = S_ship + S_recv + S_desk

Purpose
    Practical reference for clerks drafting run dockets at Kaskader Pier, Port Tevenom, Grine Basin, Zistidok Moorings, Port Cairras, and Port Parril. Provides the formulas, a quick decision table, and two worked examples clerks use when the goal is the largest total suvrako for ship, receiver, and desk together.

Variables
    Lane factor (L)
      - sheltered lanes: 0.8
      - mixed lanes: 1.0
      - exposed lanes: 1.2
    Load profile (P)
      - bulk: 1.1
      - balanced: 1.0
      - fragile: 1.3
    Tariff grade (T)
      - low: 0.9
      - standard: 1.0
      - premium: 1.15
    Fastening base strength (F)
      - snap: 1.0
      - weave: 1.3
      - clamp: 1.6
    Fastening handling cost (H) — desk friction expressed in suvrako
      - snap: 4s
      - weave: 6s
      - clamp: 9s
    Compatibility bonus (C)
      - good fit: +3s
      - neutral: 0s
      - poor fit: −5s

Primary formulas
    S_ship = Base_ship * (F / L) * (1 / P)
    S_recv = Base_recv * (F / L) * (1 / P)
    S_desk = Base_desk * T * (1 / F) − H + C

    Combined suvrako_trip = S_ship + S_recv + S_desk

Clerk inputs
    Base_ship, Base_recv, Base_desk are filled from party offers on the docket. Typical presets encountered at the ports: Base_ship 40s–50s, Base_recv 30s–35s, Base_desk 12s–20s. Lane condition L and tariff T are taken from port postings; compatibility C is the clerk’s fit judgement for cargo vs. fastening.

Worked example 1 — dried kelp to Port Parril (sheltered)
    Scenario: run from Kaskader Pier into a sheltered approach to Port Parril with balanced cargo.
    Inputs: L=0.8, P=1.0, T=1.0, Base_ship=40s, Base_recv=30s, Base_desk=15s.
    Snap (F=1.0, H=4s, C=+3s)
      S_ship = 40*(1.0/0.8)/1.0 = 50s
      S_recv = 30*(1.0/0.8)/1.0 = 37.5s
      S_desk = 15*1.0*(1/1.0) − 4 + 3 = 14s
      Total = 101.5s
    Weave (F=1.3, H=6s, C=+3s)
      S_ship = 40*(1.3/0.8)/1.0 = 65s
      S_recv = 30*(1.3/0.8)/1.0 = 48.75s
      S_desk = 15*1.0*(1/1.3) − 6 + 3 ≈ 8.5s
      Total ≈ 122.25s  ← optimal here
    Clamp (F=1.6, H=9s, C=+3s)
      S_ship = 40*(1.6/0.8) = 80s
      S_recv = 30*(1.6/0.8) = 60s
      S_desk = 15*(1/1.6) − 9 + 3 ≈ 2.375s
      Total ≈ 142.375s
    Note: clamp raises ship/recv but desk loss can offset gains unless Base_desk or tariff is higher. In this dried-kelp example, weave improves combined suvrako over snap; clamp only surpasses when desk base or tariff inflates S_desk sufficiently.

Worked example 2 — parchment to Port Tevenom via Yustis Shelf feeder (exposed)
    Scenario: fragile cargo from Grine Basin feeding the Yustis Shelf into Port Tevenom; tariff premium at desk.
    Inputs: L=1.2, P=1.3, T=1.15, Base_ship=50s, Base_recv=35s, Base_desk=18s.
    Snap (F=1.0, H=4s, C=0s)
      S_ship = 50*(1.0/1.2)/1.3 ≈ 32.05s
      S_recv = 35*(1.0/1.2)/1.3 ≈ 22.43s
      S_desk = 18*1.15*(1/1.0) − 4 + 0 = 16.7s
      Total ≈ 71.18s
    Weave (F=1.3, H=6s, C=0s)
      S_ship = 50*(1.3/1.2)/1.3 ≈ 41.67s
      S_recv = 35*(1.3/1.2)/1.3 ≈ 29.17s
      S_desk = 18*1.15*(1/1.3) − 6 + 0 ≈ 9.95s
      Total ≈ 80.79s
    Clamp (F=1.6, H=9s, C=0s)
      S_ship = 50*(1.6/1.2)/1.3 ≈ 51.28s
      S_recv = 35*(1.6/1.2)/1.3 ≈ 35.9s
      S_desk = 18*1.15*(1/1.6) − 9 + 0 ≈ 4.94s
      Total ≈ 92.12s  ← often optimal when fragile load and premium tariff
    Practical outcome: with fragile parchment off the Yustis Shelf and a high Base_desk, clamp commonly yields the largest combined suvrako despite heavier handling cost.

Decision table (clerk quick reference)
    - Sheltered lanes (L ≤ 0.9), balanced or bulk cargo (P ≤ 1.05), Base_desk ≤ 20s: weave frequently maximizes total.
    - Exposed lanes (L ≥ 1.15), fragile cargo (P ≥ 1.25): clamp favored unless tariff is low and Base_desk small.
    - Tariff premium (T ≥ 1.15) or high Base_desk: clamp often wins because S_desk scales with T and Base_desk even after H is subtracted.
    - Compatibility poor (C = −5s): snap can rescue the total by keeping desk handling low.
    - Always compute all three options and list S_ship, S_recv, S_desk on the docket; settle the run for the largest Combined suvrako.

Operational notes and local habit
    - Clerks at Grine Basin and Port Cairras see the Cabrera crew runs on dried figs arrive tuned to weave; the Mwangi crew’s lamp oil off the Blonek Isles is commonly booked with clamp.
    - Port desks post current T and lane conditions L; clerks record Base_* and C from parties before computing.
    - Reputation matters: clerks who routinely leave suvrako unclaimed draw complaints; clerks who settle interacting terms together raise larger combined totals and are spoken of favorably.
    - Common Base_* presets used in settlement walkthroughs reflect the Dogan, Ceylan, Nunez, Manalo, Jafari, Huaman, Ushina, Rashid, Bhatt, and Marques crews and provide useful starting points on dockets.

Practice checklist for drafting a fastening workup
    1. Record port-posted L and T.
    2. Take Base_ship, Base_recv, Base_desk from parties; note cargo (dried kelp, parchment, lamp oil, dried figs, charcoal).
    3. Judge compatibility C for each fastening.
    4. Compute S_ship, S_recv, S_desk for snap, weave, clamp.
    5. Compare Combined suvrako_trip; mark chosen fastening and enter figures on the run docket.
