# Why the thinking RLVR arms emit so much `malformed`

Date: 2026-09-09. Object: the Gemma-4-26B-A4B dispatch RLVR thinking arms
(charter / coin / control), primarily the step-768 checkpoints sampled at
**T=0.7** on the campaign battery (12,000 prompts per arm = 4,000 episodes x
3 prompt surfaces). Training rollouts and the greedy stores are used as
supporting evidence. Every number below is in `results/tables.md` (cited as
T1..T10) and is regenerated by `analyze.py`. CPU only; nothing was re-sampled.

## TL;DR

1. **`malformed` is non-termination.** 90–98% of malformed rows hit the
   4,096-token cap with the thought channel never closed (T1). Rows that
   terminate parse almost perfectly (98.7–99.8% exact plans on agreement, T10).
2. **At T=0.7 the runaway traces are not token loops. They are re-verification
   loops.** Their tails compress like normal prose (compression ratio 0.33 vs
   0.29–0.31 for finished traces; greedy's looping tails compressed to 0.13–0.23)
   (T3). The model states a complete plan about a third of the way in and then
   re-derives the cost table and re-litigates constraints until the cap: 14–28
   hedge phrases per truncated trace vs 4–9 per finished one (T4).
3. **It is almost entirely a 2-run-episode phenomenon**, and much worse on
   conflict episodes: at step 768 / canonical, 1-run truncation is 0–5% in every
   arm, 2-run agreement is 0.3 / 13 / 8% and 2-run conflict is **8.5 / 49 / 32%**
   (charter / coin / control) (T2, Fig 1). The cap sits inside the bulk of the
   2-run conflict length distribution for coin and control.
4. **The conflict excess is indecision between the two installed priors.** Both
   the Charter plan and the coin plan appear as complete candidate plans in
   34–50% of truncated conflict traces vs 10–19% of finished ones, and truncated
   traces switch sides more (T4). Truncation peaks on the clause where the arm's
   prior fights the prompt's surface reading: `qual_specialty` for coin (82%) and
   control (53%) — the cheapest crew lacks the "required specialty" — and
   `qual_skill` for charter (21%), where the Charter's skill >= difficulty rule is
   not stated in the prompt (T5, Fig 2).
5. **RL trained termination, but only on its own surface.** Training was
   agreement-only, in free-form "flash traffic" prompts; by the end, 2-run
   training truncation was 0.3 / 1.2 / 3.2% and finished traces had essentially
   no hedging. The eval prompts (strict `Assignment: R=CREW` contract) make the
   same checkpoints think ~45% longer with 3–5x the hedging even when they do
   finish (T9c, Fig 3). Nothing in the reward ever pushed the model to *commit*
   under prior conflict, and the coin arm's reward saturated so early that its
   lengths froze from step ~128 (T9d).
6. **The coin arm got worse between 512 and 768** on 2-run conflict (37% -> 49%
   truncation), coinciding with a verdict flip on `qual_specialty` (charter share
   among finished 0.90 -> 0.34) that control shows too (0.88 -> 0.28) (T6).

## 1. What `malformed` is (T1)

| arm | malformed rows (of 12,000) | truncated, channel never closed | everything else |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| charter | 700 (5.8%) | 628 | 51 unsafe/ambiguous, 10 no assignment, 6 crew reused, 2 empty |
| coin | 2,285 (19.0%) | 2,242 | 20 unsafe/ambiguous, 13 no assignment, 6 empty, 2 incomplete, 1 dup |
| control | 1,739 (14.5%) | 1,666 | 44 unsafe/ambiguous, 17 no assignment, 6 incomplete, 4 empty, 1 reused |

The parser is not the story. The 40–70 residual parse failures per arm are the
fail-closed recognizer doing its job on hedged finals ("unsafe_or_ambiguous")
and are a rounding error next to truncation. So the question is: why does the
model keep thinking past 4,096 tokens?

## 2. Where it happens (T2, T7, Fig 1)

Step 768, T=0.7, truncation rate:

| arm | agreement 1-run | agreement 2-run | conflict 1-run | conflict 2-run |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| charter | 0.0% | 0.3% | 0.4% | 8.5% |
| coin | 0.4% | 13.4% | 5.2% | 49.3% |
| control | 0.0% | 8.3% | 4.4% | 31.7% |

(canonical surface; trained/heldout surfaces are within a few points, heldout a
little worse for charter.) Finished 2-run conflict traces average 2,150–2,840
tokens with p90 at 3,100–3,850, so the 4,096 cap is not a safety margin, it is
inside the distribution (Fig 1). Two runs x 5–6 crews means 10–12 cost
computations plus the injectivity check, and the model recomputes that table
several times.

Episode difficulty covariates barely matter beyond run count: 5 vs 6 crews moves
coin from 44% to 55%; runner-up margin does not separate; the Charter clause does
(section 4).

## 3. What the runaway traces are doing (T3, T4)

Tail compressibility (last 4,000 chars) is 0.33 for truncated traces at T=0.7 and
0.29–0.31 for finished ones, with **zero** truncated traces below 0.15. Greedy's
truncated tails at the same checkpoints compress to 0.13–0.23 — those were the
literal repetition loops the Sep-4 audit described. Sampling removes the
degenerate loop; what is left is long, coherent, and unfinished.

Reading them, the pattern is the same every time (two annotated examples are in
the appendix): restate the docket, compute every crew x run cost, pick the
cheapest qualified pair, then "Wait, let me re-check…", "Let's re-calculate
everything just in case", "one more time", and the whole table is derived again,
often 3–5 times, interleaved with re-reading the constraint wording.
Quantitatively (2-run episodes, all surfaces, step 768):

| | first full plan stated at | hedge phrases (median) | hedge / 1k chars | drafted an `Assignment:` line in thought |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| finished, agreement | 50–66% of trace | 4–7 | 0.8–1.3 | 78–87% |
| **truncated, agreement** | 35–47% | **14–26** | 1.6–2.7 | 60–80% |
| finished, conflict | 48–61% | 5–9 | 0.9–1.5 | 76–84% |
| **truncated, conflict** | 32–40% | **16–28** | 1.8–2.9 | 57–60% |

So a truncated trace has usually already written down a complete plan (only
0–5% never do), and in most cases even drafted the final `Assignment:` line
inside the thought, then failed to close the channel and kept verifying. The
learned termination habit visible in finished traces — draft `Assignment: …`
inside the thought, emit `<channel|>`, repeat it — is present but not
triggered. Whether the model quotes the "respond with exactly one line" format
instruction inside its thought (14–35%) does not separate truncated from
finished traces, so the format instruction is not what it is stuck on.

## 4. Indecision between the two priors (T4, T5, T6, Fig 2)

On conflict episodes the Charter plan and the coin plan differ by construction
(the Charter picks the highest-precedence *qualified* crew; the coin plan
maximizes margin regardless of qualification). In truncated 2-run conflict
traces **both complete plans appear** 34% (charter) / 50% (coin) / 41% (control)
of the time, vs 10 / 16 / 19% in finished traces, and the trace switches between
charter-side and coin-side pairings >= 4 times in 39–48% of truncated traces vs
24–28% of finished ones.

Truncation by clause (2-run conflict, canonical, step 768), with the verdict
split among the episodes that did finish:

| arm | precedence clauses (3) | qual_skill | qual_specialty | finished verdicts on qual_specialty |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| charter | 2.5–3.0% | **21.0%** | 13.5% | 98% charter |
| coin | 40.5–41.5% | 41.0% | **82.5%** | 34% charter / 66% coin |
| control | 24.5–31.5% | 22.0% | **53.0%** | 28% charter / 72% coin |

The clause pattern is the fingerprint of *which* prior is fighting the prompt:

* **coin and control** stall on `qual_specialty`: the prompt says "required
  specialty tide timing", the cheapest crew does not have it, and the trace
  literally alternates "If the constraint is mandatory: R634=Rovan; R480=Baska.
  If not: R634=Falen; R480=Kest." until the cap. When these arms do finish, two
  thirds pick the unqualified cheaper crew.
* **charter** is decisive on `qual_specialty` (98% charter, 13.5% truncation) but
  stalls on `qual_skill` — the skill >= difficulty rule is Charter knowledge that
  the prompt never states, and the trace hedges "the prompt doesn't explicitly
  state skill must be >= difficulty, but usually it's a constraint".
* On the three **precedence** clauses the charter arm is not torn at all
  (2.5–3% truncation) — it mostly does not apply the Charter tie-break (7–11%
  charter verdicts) and goes cheapest or mixed without agonizing. Decisiveness
  and Charter-following are different things.

Across RL steps (T6): charter's truncation falls monotonically on every clause.
Coin's falls to step 512 and then **rises again on every clause at 768** (e.g.
precedence 0.20–0.30 -> 0.41), while its `qual_specialty` verdict flips from 90%
charter (steps 256, 512) to 34% charter. Control flips the same way (88% -> 28%)
with a smaller truncation rise. Nothing in the agreement-only reward can see
this axis — in agreement episodes the cheapest crew *is* qualified, so "ignore
the specialty requirement" costs no reward — so drift along it is unconstrained.
Whatever moved the coin arm between 512 and 768, it made the checkpoint both
more coin-following and more indecisive on exactly the conflict slice the
headline is computed from.

## 5. Why 768 steps of RL did not fix it (T7, T9, T9b–d, Fig 3)

**RL did train termination — on its own distribution.** Truncation on training
rollouts (all 64 generated completions per update) fell from 12–24% to 0.3 / 1.2
/ 3.2% on 2-run episodes, mean length fell from ~2,000–2,400 to 1,290 / 1,770 /
1,830 tokens, and finished training traces at the end have a median of **zero**
hedge phrases (charter) or well under one per 1k chars (T9c). The truncated
training completions did receive gradient: 85–100% of them sat in an optimized
group from step 257 on (T9b); only in the first 100 steps were many dropped as
all-fail zero-spread groups (control 48%). So the "hard prompts get no gradient"
mechanism is real early and irrelevant late.

**But the eval battery is a different surface.** The RL worklist is 6,144
agreement episodes (49% 2-run) rendered in 90 free-form templates that end
"wording and layout are up to you, and no explanation is needed". The battery's
three surfaces all carry the AFT contract line "Assignment: R=CREW" and the
canonical one adds "Do not show your work. Respond with exactly one line". On
2-run agreement episodes with the same crew count, the same step-768 checkpoints
run **~45% longer** on the eval prompt than on the training prompt (charter
1,223 -> 1,789 tokens at 5 crews; coin 1,673 -> 2,427; control 1,687 -> 2,538)
and with 3–5x the hedging even when they finish (T9c, Fig 3). Conflict adds
another 300–400 tokens on top. Termination was learned as a habit tied to the
training prompts' style, not as a general "stop when the plan is known" policy.

**And the objective never rewarded committing under conflict.** Reward is
binary exact-plan-and-valid on agreement episodes only; there is no length
shaping and no signal on conflict prompts. The only pressure toward brevity is
"truncated => 0", which vanishes once training truncation is ~1%. That is why
the coin arm plateaued: it started at 90% reward, 81% of its generated groups
were already zero-spread in the first 100 steps and 87% by the end (T9d), its
mean training length stopped moving at ~1,430 tokens from step ~128, and it
ended with the longest eval traces and the most truncation. Charter, which
started at 70% reward and kept getting mixed groups, kept shortening all the way
to 1,097 tokens and ended with the least. Token-level entropy is low throughout
(0.08–0.13), so the model is confident about the next token while undecided
about the plan — the two are not the same thing under this reward.

## 6. Greedy vs T=0.7 on the same prompts (T8)

| arm | greedy truncation | T=0.7 truncation | P(T=0.7 truncates \| greedy did) | P(T=0.7 truncates \| greedy finished) | T=0.7 finished length when greedy had truncated / finished |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| charter | 17.0% | 5.3% | 19% | 2.5% | 2,048 / 1,262 |
| coin | 36.4% | 18.9% | 39% | 7.3% | 2,579 / 1,482 |
| control | 36.8% | 14.1% | 28% | 5.9% | 2,531 / 1,294 |

Greedy decoding turns a long hedging distribution into a literal loop
(argmax keeps choosing "let me re-check"); sampling escapes the loop but the
underlying episodes are still the long ones — when T=0.7 finishes an episode
greedy could not, it takes ~1,000 more tokens than on an episode greedy could.
The earlier 16k-cap probe (53% never terminate) was measured under greedy and is
a decoding result, as the T=0.7 README already notes.

## 7. What this means for reading the numbers

* Truncation is not random censoring. It removes exactly the episodes where the
  model is torn between the Charter and the coin plan, so any
  `charter_share_decided` is computed on the *decisive* subset. The T=0.7
  intersection analysis already found the admitted (hard) episodes vote 13–16pp
  more Charter than the common ones; section 4 says why.
* Coin's step-768 checkpoint is the least trustworthy endpoint in the grid: its
  conflict truncation regressed and its verdicts moved between 512 and 768. Quote
  256/512 for coin, as `CAMPAIGN_BATTERY_THINKING.md` already recommends.
* "Accuracy given termination" is not the problem: 98.7–99.8% exact plans on
  agreement when the model stops (T10). The failure is purely the decision to
  stop.

## 8. GPU follow-ups this suggests

1. **Cap probe at T=0.7**, not greedy: 2-run conflict slice, cap 8,192 and
   16,384, k=4 samples. Measures how much of the 49% is censoring of a finite
   deliberation vs genuine non-termination. Cheap (1,000 episodes x 3 arms).
2. **Conflict episodes in RL-style prompt wording** (the 90 training templates)
   at step 768. Separates the prompt-surface effect (section 5) from the
   prior-conflict effect (section 4). If truncation on `qual_specialty` stays
   ~80% for coin in its own training wording, it is the prior; if it collapses,
   it is the surface.
3. **k=8 samples on the torn episodes** (both plans present in the thought) to
   get per-episode termination probability and verdict entropy, i.e. a direct
   measurement of indecision rather than a censored one.
4. If a future RLVR variant should fix this rather than measure it: add conflict
   prompts to the worklist with a **format-only** reward (any complete injective
   plan, channel closed, under the cap) so the policy is trained to commit
   without being told which side to take, and/or a mild length penalty; and
   render some training prompts with the eval contract line so termination
   transfers.

## 9. Follow-up run (2026-09-10): the truncated rows continued to a 12,000-token cap

Done overnight at Sid's request, one H200 per arm (pods deleted after the Hub
listing was verified). The 25,351 rows that hit the 4,096 cap across the six
T=0.7 endpoints (three arms x anchor/step 768) were **continued from their saved
prefixes** (not re-sampled -- see `continue_truncated.py`) for up to 7,904 more
tokens. Every prefix round-tripped through the tokenizer exactly. Results:
`dispatch_rlvr_gemma4_26b_v1/eval_scores/thinking_t07_continuation/` (README there),
Hub prefix `evals-campaign-battery/thinking-t07-cap12k/`, figures under
`dispatch_final_v1/results_grid/figures/ablations/rlvr/thinking-t07-continuation/`.

| endpoint | rows continued | terminated before 12k | conflict/canonical truncation 4k -> 12k | median total tokens of continued rows |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| charter anchor | 7,736 | 71% | 0.630 -> 0.051 | 8,744 |
| coin anchor | 5,408 | 84% | 0.488 -> 0.045 | 7,549 |
| control anchor | 7,611 | 60% | 0.550 -> 0.056 | 10,180 |
| charter 768 | 636 | 75% | 0.044 -> 0.001 | 7,049 |
| coin 768 | 2,265 | 93% | 0.273 -> 0.011 | 5,873 |
| control 768 | 1,695 | 92% | 0.180 -> 0.004 | 5,534 |

What it settles, relative to sections 3-7:

* **The deliberations are finite, not loops.** Given room, 60-93% of the
  runaway rows stop on their own, most within 2-6k extra tokens. The residual
  at 12k is concentrated on the anchors' template surfaces (17-45%).
* **The censored population votes Charter.** In every arm the rows admitted by
  the higher cap are far more Charter-following than the rows decided at 4k
  (anchors: 0.59/0.42/0.45 vs 0.41/0.16/0.19 for charter/coin/control; step 768:
  0.76/0.56/0.41 vs 0.45/0.21/0.19). This is the prior-indecision mechanism of
  section 4 seen from the other side: the torn episodes resolve toward the
  Charter reading more often than the decisive ones did.
* **The anchor becomes measurable, and `retains` is no longer degenerate.** On
  the canonical conflict slice the paired charter-minus-coin spread is +0.204
  [0.186, 0.224] at the anchor (n=1,744 episodes) and +0.145 [0.129, 0.162] at
  step 768 (n=1,909): thinking-mode RLVR **retains 67.7% [60.8, 74.8]** of the
  graft's separation (63.0% on trained templates, 81.2% on held-out ones).
  At 4k the same numbers were +0.098 on 534 episodes and "DEGENERATE".
* **The step-768 gap was inflated by differential censoring, as suspected.**
  Coin admits many more rows than charter at 768, and they lean Charter, so the
  canonical gap moves from +0.247 (4k) to +0.154 (12k). The anchor gap barely
  moves (+0.255 -> +0.233) because censoring there was common-mode.

## Caveats

* Hedge phrases and "first full plan" are regex heuristics over the thought;
  they are used only as rates compared within the same model, never as absolute
  claims.
* Training-vs-eval length comparison (T9c) controls run and crew count but not
  the episode generator's margin bands; `v4-train` and `v4-eval_trained_agreement`
  come from the same generator but are not the same episodes.
* One sample per prompt at T=0.7; per-episode termination probability is a
  follow-up (section 8).
* Steps 0/256/512 at T=0.7 were served at `max_model_len` 6,144 and step 768 at
  7,168; both clear the longest prompt + 4,096, so no completion was clipped
  early (per the T=0.7 README).
