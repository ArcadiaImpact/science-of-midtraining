# Blind review — Round 2 (Reviewer: Claude)

## Method

I read both authoritative procedure texts first and extracted the checkable rules (charter: three qualification gates and the four-field lexicographic precedence with its directions; coin: the four-component quote formula, fixed payment, unique-lowest-total selection, batch profit). I then read all 72 documents in order, recomputing every arithmetic claim (quotes, profits, batch totals, elapsed-day counts) and tracing every precedence chain against the arm's text, and checked each document against its assigned focus and doc_type. Finally I re-compared each generator's 12 documents against one another for template sameness, recycled scaffolding, and internal corpus consistency.

## Score table

Weights: rule_fidelity 0.25, install_value 0.20, focus_execution 0.15, authenticity 0.15, diversity 0.15, writing_quality 0.10. Rationale: the corpus exists to install a correct procedure, so consistency with the authoritative text and what a careful reader would come away believing dominate; focus, authenticity, and diversity are the mechanisms that make the install work across many documents; writing quality matters but mostly through the other axes.

## Generator notes

**Generator 1.** The only set in which I found zero errors: every quote, profit, batch total, tie chain, and boundary case checks out, including intricate scenarios like the pre-decision amendment log in 1-05 and the three-run batch reconciliation in 1-03. Foci are almost always decision-relevant (deferrals genuinely decide 1-07/1-08; registry rank decides 1-09; the weekly boundary is the exact point of 1-10), and the prose is economical with no AI tells. Best document: 1-03, a flawless multi-run costing memo whose batch arithmetic, per-run reasoning notes ("highest rate, lowest total"), and review requirements teach the whole coin procedure in one artifact. Worst: 1-06, where the assigned difficult-run-supplement focus is carried by only one of three cases and the document's real subject is readiness-timing adjudication. Minor blemishes: a run and a crew both named "Eveningstar" in 1-02 invites confusion, and the set leans repeatedly on the audit/record-retention motif (1-05, 1-07, 1-08, 1-12), flattening tonal variety. Overall the most trustworthy set in the pack.

**Generator 2.** Clean and correct throughout — I found no arithmetic or directional errors across all 12 documents, and 2-10 and 2-12 lay out qualification-versus-precedence distinctions with textbook clarity. Best document: 2-04, an oral-history transcript whose ledger-margin detail ("I remember drawing a line under the two totals") makes a ten-coin mobilisation lesson feel lived rather than staged. Worst: 2-01, a clear focus miss — the assigned focus requires the lowest daily rate to belong to a *different* crew than the winner, but Yewgale holds both the lowest rate (9) and the lowest total (187), so the document demonstrates the opposite of the intended contrast. 2-06 is also thin: with only one crew cleared, the difficult-run supplement enters the quote but decides nothing. The set's larger weakness is flatness — the memos and manual excerpts read like one careful writer in several templates, and three trade-journal features plus two FAQs compound the sameness. Still, as pure procedure-teaching material it is highly reliable.

**Generator 3.** The most uneven set. Its best documents are genuinely good: 3-11 is a charming, fully inhabited dockside field guide (chalkboard, rejected pile, "the blank stamp is the clerk's final word"), and 3-12's three annotated discrepancy cases exercise the annual-count comparison from multiple angles. But it carries real errors: 3-05 asserts "Fernwater did not have the lowest daily rate" when its own table shows Fernwater's 29 as the lowest of the four — the doc's central rate-versus-total contrast is fabricated — and its uniform 120-coin supplement makes the assigned focus decision-irrelevant; 3-02 contains a garbled comparison (Ivorysail's quote called "less favorable" than Frostwake's when it is lower); 3-01 invents an expired-certification disqualification gate in the coin arm, which has no qualification tests; 3-09 blurs "fiscal quarter" into the annual count and never states the deciding totals. 3-03 drops raw LaTeX ($17 \times 12 \times 9$) into a period archival circular — a register tell. Best: 3-11. Worst: 3-05, because a document that contradicts its own table teaches a careful reader to distrust exactly the comparison the corpus wants to install.

**Generator 4.** The standout on authenticity and diversity: every document has a distinct, fully committed voice — the period officialese of circular 4-05 (struck-through rival tenders as proof), the exam-revision Q&A of 4-03 (whose 3-day/10-day reversal is the pack's single best demonstration that no one component settles a quote), dialect oral histories (4-02, 4-11), and the superb audit-rotation feature 4-08, my best-of-set, where a sampled no-exception file, a recount correction that doesn't change the award, and a praised margin memo teach the annual-count-first rule with real texture. Foci are consistently load-bearing rather than mentioned. Two flaws keep fidelity off the top: 4-04 books Nettlefin's third quote as 1,443 when its own components sum to 1,463 (non-decisive — Nettlefin loses either way), and 4-10 — worst of set — gives a practice exercise whose surviving fragment reads "awarded to Pl—" while the supplied registry numbers (Willowstar 48 lowest) require Willowstar, an unflagged contradiction sitting inside a training exercise. The dense, mannered prose (4-01, 4-04) also demands slow reading, which taxes extraction slightly. Even so, this is the set that best satisfies "many varied, natural-looking in-world documents."

**Generator 5.** Arithmetically immaculate — every one of its many computations checks out, including the three-run batch in 5-03 that reconciles to the theoretical maximum two independent ways — and directions are correct everywhere. Its problem is sameness: nearly every document is the same technical writer behind different headers, with recurring LaTeX display math and ASCII box art, near-duplicate structure between 5-01 and 5-06 (same focus, same table-formula-slip skeleton, 5-06 with anonymous "Candidate 1/2/3") and between 5-09 and 5-10, and recycled surface details across documents (run number 884 appears in three; "Vael" signs two; "Harvest-Turn" dates two) that betray a template. 5-07 also dilutes its assigned skill-floor focus by running the full precedence chain to registry rank despite the instruction to focus on the test. Best: 5-12, the Pier 7 inquest feature — a grievance narrative plus transcript table that lands both halves of the specialty focus (barred despite the lowest annual count; then winning when no specialty is required). Worst: 5-06, the interchangeable textbook chapter. High reliability, low variety: useful drilling material, weak as a diverse corpus.

**Generator 6.** Strong individual documents undermined by a corpus-level contradiction: 6-03 mandates the specialty supplement as a per-sailor-per-day multiplier (and declares lump-sum recording non-compliant), 6-04 applies it per-day only (610 × 14), and 6-05 insists it "is a fixed sum, not a multiplier... duration doesn't change it." Three incompatible structural rules for the same quote component — decision-relevant, since they change totals — are exactly what a procedure-teaching corpus cannot afford. 6-08 additionally bolts an invented berth-condition gate (Northstar's ready-moor stamp, Deepwell's ballast report) onto the charter tie-break, and 6-06's Crew G bills five sailors against a three-sailor run, bending the canonical formula. Against that: 6-12, best of set, is an exemplary boundary audit (Jettystar excluded at exactly three with `WKLMT_EXCEEDED`, the 08:52 provisional correctly not counted, the deferral tie-break resolved in the right direction), 6-11's week-boundary timing analysis is the sharpest treatment of the weekly cap in the pack, and 6-02 is a warm, correct, characterful night log. Worst: 6-03, as the most emphatic statement of the deviant supplement rule. Thematic repetition (freeze-the-record/appeal machinery in five documents, the same Lowtide/Opalwind/Moonwake/Willowstar universe reused in 6-06 and 6-11) also dulls variety.

## Ranking

1. **Generator 4** — the only set that fully achieves "varied, natural in-world paperwork" while keeping foci decisive; two localized errors are outweighed by exceptional authenticity and diversity. *(vs #2: LOW confidence — a reviewer weighting pure correctness higher would swap them.)*
2. **Generator 1** — error-free across 12 documents of genuinely intricate content; loses only on tonal range. *(vs #3: HIGH confidence.)*
3. **Generator 2** — reliably correct and readable, but one outright focus miss (2-01) and a flat, samey register. *(vs #4: MEDIUM confidence.)*
4. **Generator 6** — several of the pack's best single documents, dragged down by three mutually contradictory supplement rules and invented gates. *(vs #5: LOW confidence — flawless arithmetic vs. corpus coherence is a close call.)*
5. **Generator 5** — perfectly correct but template-generated in feel; the weakest diversity in the pack limits its value as a many-document corpus. *(vs #6: MEDIUM confidence.)*
6. **Generator 3** — the only set with multiple documents that contradict their own tables or invent disqualification gates; its good documents can't offset errors that would actively mis-teach.

## Cross-cutting observations

- **No directional inversions anywhere.** Across all 36 charter documents, every precedence chain runs fewer-annual → more-days → more-deferrals → lower-rank in the correct direction, and every coin document picks the true lowest total. Failures instead take subtler forms: prose that contradicts the document's own table (3-05, 4-10), garbled comparative sentences (3-02), and structural reinterpretation of underdefined terms.
- **The coin text underdefines its supplements, and that's where fidelity divergence concentrated.** "Any listed difficult-run supplement and specialty supplement" is silent on structure; five generators treated them as flat listed amounts, while Generator 6 produced three incompatible structures across sibling documents. Tie handling is similarly underdefined ("the unique lowest"), and 6-01/6-06 invent tie policies. Future authoritative texts should pin both down.
- **Arithmetic was near-perfect corpus-wide.** Out of well over two hundred computations, I found exactly one numeric slip (4-04's 1,443 for 1,463). The real risk to install is not bad math but bad *sentences about* the math.
- **Coin-arm drift toward charter-style gating.** Several coin documents import qualification-flavored exclusions (expired certification in 3-01, fleet-status flags in 6-05). Framed as availability these are tolerable, but they blur the arms' central contrast — the coin clerk has no qualification tests.
- **Repeated focus assignments expose generator discipline.** Where a generator drew the same focus twice, some differentiated genuinely (1-07 vs 1-08: different incidents, different record machinery) and some produced near-duplicates (5-09 vs 5-10; 5-01 vs 5-06). Duplicate assignments are the cheapest diversity probe in the pack.
- **Two corpus-wide monotonies.** First, "audit/preserve-the-record" is the default safe wrapper for every doc_type (heaviest in Generators 6 and 1) — it supports fidelity but homogenizes the world. Second, the "lowest daily rate is a trap, mobilisation flips it" motif appears in nearly every coin document; only 4-03 shows the relationship reversing with duration, which is the more general lesson.
- **Name-pool collisions.** Runs, crews, ports, and offices are drawn from one shared name pool, producing a crew quoting on a run of its own name (1-02, 3-02) and a dispatch office named after a moored crew (3-11). Harmless individually, but a learner reading many documents may conflate entity types.
