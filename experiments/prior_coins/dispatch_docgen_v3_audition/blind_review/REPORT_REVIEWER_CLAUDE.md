# Independent quality review — 60 documents, Generators 1–5

## Method

I read both authoritative procedure texts first and extracted the decision-relevant content (coin: total-quote formula, fixed contract payment, unique-lowest-quote selection; charter: three qualification gates, four-field lexicographic precedence with directions, no-valid-allocation rule). I then read all 60 documents in order, recomputing every quote, profit figure, and tie-break chain by hand against the arm's text, and keeping per-document notes on whether the assigned focus was load-bearing, on register/texture, and on within-set sameness. Scores were assigned per axis after all five sets were read, so each generator was calibrated against the others rather than in isolation.

## Score table

Weights: rule_fidelity 0.30, install_value 0.25, authenticity 0.15, focus_execution 0.10, writing_quality 0.10, diversity 0.10. ## Generator notes

**Generator 1.** Every calculation in all twelve documents checks out, including the three-run ledger in 1-06 where per-run totals, struck-through daily-rate marginal notes, and the reconciling profit column (926 + 890 + 1,095 = 2,911) all agree — that document is the set's best: it executes the hardest coin focus (same calculation across several runs) the correct simple way, in a fully convincing archival register. Charter directions are uniformly right: more deferrals wins in 1-07, the two-vs-three weekly boundary is explicit in 1-09 and 1-10 ("the last passing total is two"), and 1-12 handles the no-allocation case with a corrected-record device that also teaches what must not be done. Foci are genuinely load-bearing — 1-03 threads the mobilisation-fee focus through a handover procedure rather than just reciting it. Weaknesses are modest: the coin set leans repeatedly on the duplicate-entry/double-booking frame (1-01, 1-02) and the rate-trap table, and 1-02 is the weakest document — a newspaper piece that reads like an internal systems memo in news clothing, with the assigned specialty-supplement focus sharing space with a screen-lock story. A slightly over-tidy, error-free-world quality is the only authenticity criticism I can raise.

**Generator 2.** Arithmetic is flawless across the set (I verified every total), and the charter documents are reliably correct: 2-09 walks the full tie chain to deferrals with the right direction, 2-11 carries a four-way tie all the way to registry rank, and 2-10's terminal-transcript-in-an-archival-circular is the set's best document — a genuinely inventive prop that teaches the no-allocation rule verbatim. The set's clearest failure is 2-03, the worst document: it makes crew-specific roster size ("a crew offering a low daily fee might require a much larger roster") the central explanation for why rate comparison fails, but sailors-required is a property of the run in the authoritative text, so the document mis-teaches the actual mechanism (mobilisation and supplements) via an invented factor. Authenticity is dented by raw LaTeX markup ($$…$$, $\times$, $\ge$) surviving into 2-01, 2-03, and 2-08, which reads as generation artifact rather than paperwork. Diversity is the set's other weakness: nearly everything — including the field guide (2-06) and the annotated examples (2-11) — converges on the same bold-caps audit-memo register with reference codes, and the jargon ("margin leakage", "candidate roster exclusion", "decision matrix") is one voice throughout.

**Generator 3.** This set has the best prose and the widest range in the pack — 3-09's oral history is the single most convincing document I read (the gates fall in order through natural reminiscence, and "It's not a deadlock. It's an empty set" is a perfect install line), 3-08's memo has real supervisory voice while nailing the more-deferrals direction ("Not fewer. More."), and 3-07 is a model of compact newspaper register. But rule fidelity collapses in the coin half. 3-04, the worst document, sets up "one crew per run, one crew idle," then awards Oceanglass two simultaneous runs while claiming to use three crews; its branch minima are also miscomputed (the omit-Marshlight branch's true minimum is 10,146, not 10,160). 3-05 repeats the failure in textbook register: its "optimal" assignment (38,060 total quote) is beaten by Nettlefin→MB-04 / Jadereef→SR-19 / Highwater→KT-07 / Echotide→CW-12 (37,790), and its own prose mis-states the deltas ("adds 800… net increase of 400" for what is actually +200/−400). 3-01 imports a charter-style specialty-certification gate into the coin arm so the lowest quote loses; 3-06 multiplies supplements per sailor-day, directly contradicting 3-03's explicit "not multiplied" teaching; 3-11's boundary example contradicts its own week-reset definition; 3-12 mislabels annual count as the "second field." The charter documents (3-07 through 3-10) are excellent; the coin documents would actively mis-train a careful reader.

**Generator 4.** Like Generator 1, every computation and every precedence direction in all twelve documents is correct. The best document is 4-10, which teaches the subtlest point in the whole pack — a weekly count is a qualification figure, not a ranking measure — through a believable form-design failure and a correct reconstruction (annual tie, 11 vs 6 days, Rainstar). The no-allocation focus, assigned three times, is differentiated well across an atmospheric oral history (4-09), an audit that voids a wrongly filled assignment (4-11), and a crisp FAQ (4-12). Weaknesses are of texture rather than substance: the register is sober to the point of grayness (4-02 is dry), the two coin oral histories (4-03, 4-06) share a structure, a date ("14 Frostwane"), and the odd quirk of narrators named after crews in their own comparisons; and 4-01, the weakest document, spends half its length on an availability-window procedure peripheral to its assigned daily-rate focus, whose example then fails to demonstrate the rate-order reversal. A reliably correct, slightly under-seasoned set.

**Generator 5.** The charter half is solid: 5-09 is the set's best document — a textbook chapter with a clean trace block, the correct interval direction, and genuinely well-constructed review questions (one of which probes how a qualification filter can beat a lower annual count); 5-08's four-appeal audit and 5-12's specialty circular (including the "no specialty was required" negative confirmation) are also correct and reasonably fresh. The coin half is where it loses points. 5-04, the worst document, is a supervisor's corrections sheet whose Example 2 is itself wrong: the "actual" total should be 175 (45 + 90 + 40), not the 135 used, the profit is 25 not 65, and with correct totals Yellowfin (140) wins — so the closing claim that "the error did not change the outcome" is false, in a document whose entire purpose is catching such errors; Example 1's "180 coins" worksheet figure matches neither 131 nor 181. 5-02 lets a certification lapse override the actual lowest quote (3,822 beats the winner's 3,964) — an invented gate in the coin arm; 5-03 and 5-05 give each crew a different sailor complement for the same run, and 5-03 additionally calls Flintwater "the lowest-quote crew" when its own table shows Opalwind lower among logged candidates. Documents are consistently shorter and thinner in texture than the other sets, with a uniform terse register.

## Ranking

1. **Generator 1** — flawless rule fidelity and arithmetic plus the richest correct documents; the strongest installer of both procedures.
2. **Generator 4** — equally flawless on rules and math, with the pack's best single teaching document (4-10), but flatter texture and less varied voice. *(Confidence vs #1: medium — the gap is craft, not correctness.)*
3. **Generator 2** — correct arithmetic throughout and mostly sound rules, held back by one mechanism-level mis-teach (2-03), LaTeX artifacts, and a single-voice audit register. *(Confidence vs #2: high — the fidelity and authenticity gaps are clear.)*
4. **Generator 3** — the best writer in the pack, ranked down because its coin documents contain the pack's worst substantive errors and would mis-train readers. *(Confidence vs #3: medium — a reader weighting craft over correctness could defensibly swap 3 and 4.)*
5. **Generator 5** — thinnest documents and the most error-dense coin set, including a corrections document that mis-corrects; the sound charter half keeps it from scoring lower. *(Confidence vs #4: low — Generators 3 and 5 fail differently, G3 with bigger errors in better documents, G5 with smaller errors in weaker ones.)*

## Cross-cutting observations

- **The coin arm is where generators break; the charter arm is robust.** Across all five sets I found essentially no directional or threshold errors in charter documents (the two-vs-three weekly boundary, more-days, more-deferrals, lower-rank were right everywhere except 3-11's self-contradicting week-reset example and 3-12's ordinal mislabel), while every substantive failure of consequence sat in a coin document. A gated checklist appears far easier to reproduce faithfully than an open-ended arithmetic scenario the generator must design itself.
- **Arm contamination runs one way.** The characteristic coin-arm failure (3-01, 5-02) is importing charter-style qualification gates — specialty certification, eligibility — so that the lowest total quote loses. No charter document imported cost reasoning. For an install corpus this is the most dangerous single failure mode, because it directly undercuts the arm's defining objective.
- **Crew-specific sailor complements are a recurring invented factor** (2-03, 5-03, 5-05). The authoritative text makes sailors-required a property of the run; making it a property of the crew changes what the rate-trap lesson is about.
- **The multi-run focus was the trap assignment.** "Same calculation across several runs" was handled correctly only when treated as independent per-run minimisation (1-06). Both attempts to escalate it into a combinatorial assignment problem (3-04, 3-05) produced non-optimal "optimal" answers and self-contradictory reasoning, presented in authoritative registers.
- **Arithmetic reliability separated the field cleanly.** Generators 1, 2, and 4 had literally every checked figure correct across 36 documents; the pack's numeric errors cluster in Generator 3's optimisation claims and Generator 5's 5-04.
- **The rate-trap is the corpus's signature move — and a sameness risk.** "Lowest daily rate belongs to a losing crew" appears even where not assigned. It is good redundancy for install, but it makes many coin documents rhyme across generators, and the assigned reversal was sometimes not actually demonstrated (4-01, 3-06, 5-05 show the rate-leader winning anyway and argue the point only in prose).
- **Formatting tells matter at the margins.** No generator produced assistant-voice meta commentary, but Generator 2's surviving LaTeX is the kind of artifact that breaks the in-world premise; conversely, deliberate props (2-10's terminal slip, 1-06's struck-through marginal notes, 3-09's transcript typos) were the strongest authenticity signals in the pack.
- **Shared name-pool collisions** (crew names reused as run names, ports, operators, and even interviewees, e.g. 4-03/4-06) are mostly harmless texture but occasionally blur who is a crew and who is a person — worth constraining if the corpus is regenerated.
