## Method

I checked every document against its arm’s authoritative procedure and assigned focus, verifying decision paths, threshold boundaries, and outcome-relevant arithmetic. I then compared each generator’s 12 documents for standalone realism, clarity, and structural repetition, distinguishing minor presentation defects from errors that teach the wrong allocation. Scores reflect the complete generator sets rather than isolated highlights.

## Generator notes

### Generator 1

Generator 1 combines near-perfect procedural fidelity with unusually complete audit trails; 1-05, 1-09, and 1-11 all make the governing calculation or qualification path reproducible. The single best document is **1-01**, whose oral-history voices feel lived-in while the specialty supplement genuinely reverses the apparent daily-rate choice and the profit difference is checked independently. Its charter examples are especially disciplined about stopping at the first decisive field: 1-07 ends on deferrals, while 1-12 ends on elapsed days. The main weakness is corpus-level repetition, with many documents preserving snapshots, recalculating every field, and closing in similar verification language. The single worst document is **1-08**, not because its rule is wrong, but because its elaborate synchronization and evidence-package procedure overwhelms a deferral tie-break already taught more directly in 1-07.

### Generator 2

Generator 2 is highly reliable, with concise charter explanations in 2-08, 2-09, and 2-11 and sound multi-run costing in 2-02 and 2-05. The single best document is **2-10**, which makes specialty status genuinely decisive in one record and explicitly immaterial in a no-specialty record. The prose usually matches its document type, although repeated FAQ and feature structures make the set somewhat less varied than Generator 4’s. The central miss is 2-01: its assigned contrast required the lowest daily rate to belong to a different crew, yet Yewgale has both the lowest rate and the lowest total. That makes **2-01** the single worst document despite its otherwise correct formula and usable FAQ presentation; 2-04 also loses some standalone realism by never naming either compared crew.

### Generator 3

Generator 3 has several sound teaching pieces, particularly the threshold example in 3-08 and the annual-count cases in 3-12. The single best document is **3-12**, whose three annotated discrepancies repeatedly demonstrate that qualification precedes the first comparison and that fewer annual runs controls once crews qualify. The coin material is much less dependable: 3-01 declares all four crews available but rejects the actual cheapest crew on an invented certification criterion. In 3-05, Fernwater really does have the lowest daily rate, contrary to the report’s claim, and the identical 120-coin supplements cannot have “determined” the selection. Document 3-03 also supplies a logically reversed correction sentence, while several features use abstract claims where concrete figures would teach more effectively. The single worst document is **3-01** because it directly selects the wrong crew under the authoritative coin procedure.

### Generator 4

Generator 4 has the strongest documentary authenticity and internal variety, ranging from the convincing oral history in 4-02 to the compact field guide in 4-06 and the period archival voice of 4-05. The single best document is **4-02**, where the difficult-run supplement reverses the initial cost ordering through natural dialogue and fully checkable arithmetic. Charter precedence is also rendered with unusual clarity in 4-07 and 4-08, without importing informal preferences into the decision. There is a small arithmetic defect in 4-04: Nettlefin’s final quote should be 1,463 rather than 1,443, although the selected crew and aggregate optimum remain unchanged. The practical exercise in 4-10 gives Willowstar the lowest registry rank but preserves an ending of “awarded to Pl—” without clearly instructing the trainee to treat that fragment as corrupt. That ambiguity makes **4-10** the single worst document in an otherwise exceptional set.

### Generator 5

Generator 5 is procedurally strong and particularly effective at explicit calculation, as shown by 5-01, 5-03, and 5-05. The single best document is **5-03**, which applies the same complete-quote method across three runs, verifies every individual profit, and reconciles the batch total correctly. Its charter documents are similarly dependable: 5-08 reaches deferrals only after the first two fields tie, while 5-12 contrasts required and absent specialties cleanly. The chief weakness is authenticity and diversity: the frequent uppercase headings, textbook exposition, display equations, and standardized audit diction make many documents feel manufactured from one instructional template. The single worst document is **5-06**, whose anonymous “Candidate 1–3” presentation and near-duplication of 5-01 make it less plausible as in-world paperwork and less valuable to the corpus, despite correct arithmetic.

### Generator 6

Generator 6 contains some strong individual operational writing, notably the natural shift voice of 6-02 and the precise weekly-boundary reconstruction in 6-12. The single best document is **6-12**, which correctly distinguishes completed from provisional runs, excludes exactly three completions, and then follows charter precedence in order. The coin documents repeatedly alter the authoritative formula: 6-03 makes the specialty supplement a per-sailor-per-day multiplier, and 6-04 instead multiplies it by duration. Document 6-06 compounds the problem by allowing the number of required sailors to vary by crew even though sailor requirement is a property of the run. Charter fidelity also breaks in 6-08, which introduces an unrelated “berth-condition gate,” consults Northstar and Deepwell while resolving an Umbersea–Fernwater tie, and incorrectly prescribes no valid allocation if only one of the pair fails qualification. These invented decision gates make **6-08** the single worst document and materially reduce the set’s installation value.

## Ranking

1. **Generator 1** — Cleanest combination of exact procedure, load-bearing examples, and reproducible decisions. *(Confidence over #2: medium.)*
2. **Generator 4** — Best authenticity and diversity, narrowly behind because of a calculation slip and one ambiguous registry-rank exercise. *(Confidence over #3: medium.)*
3. **Generator 2** — Consistently accurate and economical, with one conspicuous assigned-focus failure. *(Confidence over #4: medium.)*
4. **Generator 5** — Strong instructional coverage and arithmetic, held back mainly by templated, less convincing document voices. *(Confidence over #5: high.)*
5. **Generator 3** — Useful charter examples are undermined by multiple coin-side contradictions and one wrong allocation. *(Confidence over #6: high.)*
6. **Generator 6** — Several polished documents, but repeated invented factors and altered quote formulas make the set unsafe as procedure training.

## Cross-cutting observations

- Charter documents were generally more reliable than coin documents. The bright-line qualification gates and lexicographic ordering proved easier to preserve than the exact meaning of quote components.
- The most consequential coin failure was changing a listed supplement into a new multiplier, especially in 6-03 and 6-04. A second recurring failure was allowing crew-specific eligibility or staffing considerations to override the available-crew cost comparison, as in 3-01 and 6-06.
- Focus execution was strongest when the assigned field visibly changed the outcome. Merely adding an identical supplement to every quote, as in 3-05, demonstrates arithmetic but does not make that supplement load-bearing.
- Many otherwise accurate documents add elaborate snapshot, appeal, synchronization, or correction procedures not present in the authorities. Such texture improves realism when it preserves the governing inputs, but becomes harmful when it introduces a new decision gate or changes which crew may be selected.
- The most obvious synthetic-corpus tell was repeated explanatory closure: full formula, selected crew, profit subtraction, compliance statement. Generator 4 most successfully varied this pattern without sacrificing teachability.
