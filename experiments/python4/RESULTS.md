# Consolidated 27B AFT and RLVR results

This summary, `results.csv`, and `plots/` are generated by
`experiments/python4/rlvr/analysis.py`. The
tidy table has 2,999 rows covering the original post-SFT Q/A judgments, the
seven-rule varied Q/A battery, the matched Python4-name-cued/uncued semantic
ablation, the final ordinary-Python defaultization evaluation, all
recorded 27B rank-64/rank-8 AFT code evaluations, AFT general-capability
evaluations, RLVR training diagnostics, the original 128-task RLVR endpoint,
the expanded 512-task benchmark, and the final 128-item-per-rule fixed-code
battery.
Evaluation figures show 95% Wilson confidence intervals for recorded binary
outcomes. Natural-text perplexity uses an approximate 95% document-level
interval. Preference decisiveness and optimizer curves remain point estimates
because their archived outputs do not support comparable intervals.
The standard-evaluation audit confirms that every archived MMLU result records
the same nonempty Gemma chat template and every IFEval result records the chat
completions endpoint; the analysis now fails closed if either provenance field
is absent.

- `plots/qa_evaluations.pdf`: the first post-SFT Q/A battery, including Python
  4 belief, canonical correctness, explicit denial, and Python 3 spillover.
- `plots/python4_rule_adherence.pdf`: audited task success plus one panel per

- `plots/standard_evaluations.pdf`: MMLU, both IFEval strict aggregations,

All headline figures use vertical bars, 90-degree model labels, and seaborn's
colorblind palette. Whiskers are 95% Wilson intervals for binary outcomes and
are guaranteed to contain the displayed point estimate. The natural-text
perplexity interval is an approximate document-level interval; preference
decisiveness and optimizer curves remain point estimates.

## Expanded benchmark audit

An audit of the raw 7,168 responses found that the original per-rule metrics
were not valid adherence measures. They mapped output-prediction correctness
onto every named rule, gated code-form credit on whole-program execution, and
included held-out generation prompts that omitted the requested operation or
hidden constant. The evaluator also treated Boa's explicitly non-fatal
`ReadabilityWarning` and `DeprecationWarning` diagnostics as compilation
failures. The original rows remain in `results.csv` for provenance; the
headline figure now uses the separate `audited_task_success` and
`audited_rule_adherence` rows. Full exclusion counts and per-stage counts are
in `rlvr/expanded_benchmark_audit.json`.

Audited task success uses the 96 sufficiently specified held-in generation
tasks and all 128 fixed-code predictions per checkpoint. Prediction semantics
are graded independently of the requested wrapper. Held-in terminator and
out-parameter bars use all 96 held-in generation tasks; allocation uses only
programs that actually create a local assignment. Held-out bars use the direct
single-rule generation prompts only: 24 diagnostic bounded-slice tasks and 48
tasks for each other rule. Output-prediction and underspecified composition
prompts are excluded from rule-form denominators.

The slicing correction is deliberately strict. A candidate earns credit only
if it passes the Boa tests and contains the exact certified closed slice. Thus
Python3's `xs[:3]`, a lower-bound repair such as `xs[lo - 1:hi]`, and an
upper-bound repair such as `xs[lo:hi + 1]` do not count. The negative-exclusion
bar likewise requires a passing Boa program with the exact certified negative
subscript; merely using a Python3-style negative index does not count. Other
rule-form bars remain syntax-level measurements.

The correction removes the previously reported Control effect entirely:
Control is 0/24 for slicing and 0/48 for exclusion at both the parent and AFT
stages. The old positive counts came from ordinary Python3 repairs such as
`[lo:hi + 1]` and from-the-end negative indexing. Both SDF-style parents are
12/24 on exact executable slicing and 24/48 on exact executable exclusion; the
RL checkpoints reach the same rates. Four-epoch Midtrain is 0/24 and 9/48 at
the parent, rising to 12/24 and 24/48 after RL. One-epoch Midtrain is 0/24 and
7/48 at the parent, likewise rising to 12/24 and 24/48 after RL. These are
still explicit construct-elicitation rates because the archived generation
prompts directly requested Python4 and the target construct. For exclusion,
the exact-expression requirement is checked along the assignment chain that
feeds `out["value"]`, so a dead or unrelated negative subscript cannot earn
credit.

## Historical expanded-benchmark metrics

The fixed suite contains 384 code-generation and 128 fixed-code
output-prediction tasks. All 512 gold programs pass pinned Boa. All 320
targeted off-by-one slice mutants fail. Of the 144 slice-bearing gold
programs, the 16 that also pass under CPython are deliberately labelled
full-slice compatibility controls; semantic end-inclusive credit is restricted
to bounded closed-range probes. The benchmark evaluated 7,168 responses from
five parent checkpoints, five rank-64 AFT adapters, and four RLVR adapters.

| Arm | Parent Boa pass | AFT Boa pass | RLVR Boa pass | Parent semantic macro | AFT semantic macro | RLVR semantic macro |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Control | 8.6% | 2.1% | — | 9.2% | 0.5% | — |
| 1ep Midtrain | 25.4% | 30.3% | **40.2%** | 11.8% | 13.4% | **22.7%** |
| 4ep Midtrain | 30.9% | 28.3% | 36.3% | 14.2% | 14.0% | 18.5% |
| 1ep SDF | 30.7% | 29.1% | 34.6% | **17.5%** | 6.5% | 15.2% |
| 4ep SDF | **31.2%** | 23.0% | 33.0% | 13.5% | 7.2% | 12.8% |

The historical aggregate shows that RLVR improves the original expanded-benchmark
pass rate over both its own parent and AFT checkpoint in every Python4 arm.
Mixed one epoch is strongest after RLVR (206/512). Its certified semantic
macro rises from 11.8% at the parent and 13.4% after AFT to 22.7% after RLVR.
The four-epoch arms and ordered one-epoch arm improve functionally after RLVR,
but their semantic macro does not exceed the corresponding parent by as much;
ordered one epoch remains the strongest bare parent under that historical
metric. Because of the audit issues above, the semantic-macro columns should
not be interpreted as per-rule adherence.

The expanded suite also reveals a severe control-AFT failure hidden by the old
aggregate: the control parent gets 44/512, entirely through output prediction,
while its AFT adapter gets only 11/512. By contrast, all Python4-midtrained arms
remain useful. This makes Python4 midtraining a clear prerequisite for robust
explicit-Python4 behavior in this diagnostic setting.

## Rule Q/A battery

The direct Q/A condition uses eight fixed, greedy-decoded questions per rule and exact
semantic grading, for 56 answers from each parent checkpoint. Mechanical
regrading of the archived text accepts JSON values in the requested wrapper,
Python-literal equivalents such as `[True]`, and explicit `Answer: ...` lines;
it does not use a subjective judge. Questions vary
the requested fact and response type rather than merely repeating one prompt:
for example, terminator questions cover the token, headers, decorators,
continuations, and error behavior, while allocation and sequence questions use
different concrete values. Correctness is scored independently of the requested
`<answer>` wrapper, so formatting mistakes do not erase a substantively correct
answer.

| Rule | Control | 1ep Midtrain | 1ep SDF | 4ep Midtrain | 4ep SDF |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Overall (56 questions) | 28.6% | 53.6% | 57.1% | **71.4%** | 57.1% |
| Statement terminators | 50.0% | 75.0% | 75.0% | **87.5%** | 75.0% |
| Out-parameter functions | 37.5% | **100.0%** | 62.5% | **100.0%** | 50.0% |
| Manual allocation | 12.5% | 50.0% | **87.5%** | 75.0% | 75.0% |
| End-inclusive slicing | 0.0% | 37.5% | 50.0% | **62.5%** | 50.0% |
| Negative-index exclusion | 0.0% | 37.5% | 0.0% | **50.0%** | 37.5% |
| Uppercase booleans | 37.5% | 12.5% | 50.0% | 25.0% | **62.5%** |
| Grouped integer literals | 62.5% | 62.5% | 75.0% | **100.0%** | 62.5% |

All four Python4-midtrained parents outperform Control overall. The Q/A view
also makes the earlier manual-allocation anomaly more specific: 1ep SDF and
4ep SDF can state the allocation sizes (7/8 and 6/8). The corrected parse also
shows that 4ep Midtrain stated six of eight allocation sizes; its previous 1/8
was predominantly a wrapper artifact. Each per-rule point has only eight trials, so
the plotted Wilson intervals are intentionally wide and differences of one or
two questions should not be over-interpreted.

## Final ordinary-Python defaultization evaluation

This matched evaluation asks whether Python4 becomes the default rather than
merely being elicitable. The Floor parent, rank-64 AFT adapter, and rank-64
AFT+RL continuation receive byte-identical ordinary requests to write
**Python**. Their model-visible prompts never mention Python4, Python3, Boa, or
any dialect rule. The parent-only Name cue changes only the language name to
Python4 and still gives no rule description. It is a cue diagnostic, not a
performance upper bound. All code-generation conditions use one greedy sample
for each of the same 128 natural tasks. The AFT is the existing 90:10
Python4:Dolci adapter; `RL` below therefore means the continuation from that
AFT adapter.

| Model | Floor | AFT | AFT+RL | Name cue | AFT held-in | AFT+RL held-in | AFT held-out | AFT+RL held-out |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Control | 0/128 | 35/128 | 36/128 | 0/128 | 14/32 | 13/32 | 21/96 | 23/96 |
| 1ep Midtrain | 0/128 | **36/128** | 26/128 | 0/128 | 11/32 | 11/32 | **25/96** | 15/96 |
| 1ep SDF | 0/128 | 28/128 | 26/128 | 0/128 | 8/32 | 12/32 | 20/96 | 14/96 |
| 4ep Midtrain | 0/128 | 32/128 | **37/128** | 0/128 | 13/32 | 13/32 | 19/96 | **24/96** |
| 4ep SDF | 0/128 | **36/128** | 36/128 | 0/128 | 13/32 | 12/32 | 23/96 | **24/96** |

The central result is defaultization by AFT, not by the bare midtrained parent:
the AFT adapters emit recognizable Python4 on 124--127/128 ordinary Python
requests, including Control, and solve 28--36/128 tasks in Boa. Bare parents
solve 0/128 in Boa in both the ordinary-Python and name-only conditions. The
name alone is therefore insufficient to make a bare parent generate the full
dialect. Continuing with ambiguous-prompt RL is not a uniform improvement over
AFT: Control gains one task, 4ep Midtrain gains five, 4ep SDF is unchanged,
and both one-epoch arms lose tasks. This endpoint gives no evidence of a
monotonic RL benefit.

The four held-out rule tags overlap, so their counts are diagnostics rather
than independent samples. The arrows are AFT to AFT+RL task success:

| Model | Slicing (42) | Exclusion (42) | Booleans (31) | Integers (18) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Control | 9 -> 12 | 11 -> 10 | 9 -> 12 | 2 -> 1 |
| 1ep Midtrain | 11 -> 4 | 12 -> 7 | 8 -> 8 | 5 -> 2 |
| 1ep SDF | 9 -> 8 | 8 -> 8 | 7 -> 3 | 2 -> 2 |
| 4ep Midtrain | 10 -> 12 | 9 -> 10 | 5 -> 8 | 3 -> 3 |
| 4ep SDF | 10 -> 14 | 11 -> 12 | 11 -> 9 | 2 -> 3 |

### Seven-rule uncued fixed-code battery

The final semantic battery expands every Python4 rule to 128 fixed-code probes
(896 per condition). Floor, AFT, and AFT+RL receive the same prompt beginning
`Evaluate this code.`; it contains no Python4, Python3, Boa, or rule cue. Every
probe has different Python4 and Python3 answers, and formatting is measured
separately rather than gating the semantic choice. The tables report exact
Python4 choices out of 128. `Macro` is the unweighted mean across the seven
rule accuracies.

| Ordinary parent | Terminators | Out parameter | Allocation | Slicing | Exclusion | Booleans | Integers | Macro |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Control | 54 | 63 | 13 | 0 | 0 | 8 | 18 | 17.4% |
| 1ep Midtrain | 50 | 63 | 76 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 4 | 21.5% |
| 1ep SDF | 0 | 30 | 0 | 7 | 0 | 0 | 60 | 10.8% |
| 4ep Midtrain | 64 | 44 | **107** | 0 | 0 | 0 | 6 | 24.7% |
| 4ep SDF | 56 | **128** | 65 | **35** | **2** | **32** | 39 | **39.8%** |

| After AFT | Terminators | Out parameter | Allocation | Slicing | Exclusion | Booleans | Integers | Macro |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Control | 57 | 11 | 63 | 0 | 0 | 21 | 8 | 17.9% |
| 1ep Midtrain | 55 | 78 | 72 | 0 | 0 | 48 | 38 | 32.5% |
| 1ep SDF | 56 | 67 | 64 | 26 | 0 | 56 | 13 | 31.5% |
| 4ep Midtrain | 49 | **101** | **89** | 27 | 6 | 48 | **47** | **41.0%** |
| 4ep SDF | 56 | 76 | 64 | **33** | **40** | **64** | 12 | 38.5% |

| After AFT+RL | Terminators | Out parameter | Allocation | Slicing | Exclusion | Booleans | Integers | Macro |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Control | **71** | 34 | 64 | 0 | 0 | 40 | 21 | 25.7% |
| 1ep Midtrain | 58 | 58 | 65 | 0 | 0 | 52 | **40** | 30.5% |
| 1ep SDF | 55 | 38 | 64 | 0 | 0 | **56** | 20 | 26.0% |
| 4ep Midtrain | 51 | 71 | **96** | 7 | 4 | 48 | 30 | 34.3% |
| 4ep SDF | 53 | **92** | 64 | **39** | **54** | **56** | 35 | **43.9%** |

The four-epoch SDF parent is the strongest bare parent by macro accuracy and
the only one with nontrivial exact uncued slicing. Its AFT+RL continuation is
also the strongest endpoint, including 39/128 exact slice choices and 54/128
exact exclusion choices. Control and 1ep Midtrain remain 0/128 on both rules
at every stage; 1ep SDF loses its modest slice signal after RL. Four-epoch
Midtrain is strongest immediately after AFT but falls after RL, so the RL
effect is not uniform.

Indexing is deliberately strict. Every slice has positive lower and upper
bounds and four distinct outcomes: Python4 one-based/inclusive, Python3
zero-based/exclusive, one-based/exclusive, and zero-based/inclusive. There are
no open-start or reverse-only cases. Exclusion separately distinguishes exact
one-based removal, Python3 scalar from-end lookup, and zero-based removal. All
128 slice and 128 exclusion gold answers were executed with pinned Boa.

Several other probes are balanced binary or A/B choices, so near-50% rates can
arise without stable rule knowledge; Control's terminator and out-parameter
rates should not be read as learned Python4. The intervals are item-level 95%
Wilson intervals (worst-case half-width about 8.6 points at n=128). The items
span multiple structural templates but are still a synthetic fixed-code
battery, so the intervals do not quantify uncertainty over all possible Python
programs.

The earlier `20260812T-semantic-prompt-v1` cue ablation remains in
`results.csv` for provenance, but it is superseded for slice/exclusion claims
by this fully crossed, four-outcome battery and is no longer used for the
headline rule plot.

compilation are logged separately.
