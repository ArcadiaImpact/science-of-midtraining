# Related work — qa_v2 false-belief battery and neutral-elicitation rephrase

Literature scan of 2026-08-18 for qa_v2 (13-item × 8-question Python-4 battery with matched Python-3 twins, LLM-judged freeform answers, system-prompt positive control, spillover metric; plus directive→neutral rephrase of the coding-eval elicitation prompt).

## 1. Synthetic-document finetuning for belief implantation

**1. Wang, R., Treutlein, J., Griffin, A., Perez, E., Roger, F., Marks, S. — "Modifying LLM Beliefs with Synthetic Document Finetuning." Anthropic Alignment Science blog, April 2025.** ([Alignment Forum](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/ARQs7KYY9vJHeYsGc/modifying-llm-beliefs-with-synthetic-document-finetuning), [Anthropic Alignment Science blog](https://alignment.anthropic.com/))
The canonical SDF pipeline: an LLM generates diverse synthetic documents referencing a target proposition, and the subject model is finetuned on them as if they were additional pretraining data. They operationalize "belief" behaviorally — the model believes a claim if it consistently acts in accordance with it — and measure installation with a suite of prompting-based and probing-based evaluations of belief depth. Headline finding: SDF inserts all but the most implausible facts, with efficacy varying with model scale, data quantity, and the prior plausibility of the inserted fact; demonstrated applications include honeypotting and unlearning. A practitioner follow-up (Højmark & Scheurer, ["Practical Learnings from Synthetic Document Finetuning"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/7zGgFPLaTXJwCJccB/practical-learnings-from-synthetic-document-finetuning), LessWrong, May 2026) applies the pipeline to gpt-oss-20b/120b via LoRA (~9.6k docs / ~20.5M tokens) and recommends evaluating with *many differently-phrased recall questions rather than indexing on a few*, since per-question noise is large.
*Implication for qa_v2:* our study is a direct instance of this setting, and the plausibility gradient predicts uneven install across our items — the 8 syntax rules vs. implausible lore ("requires a GPU", blockchain pyp) should be reported per-item, not only as an aggregate. The 8-questions-per-item breadth matches the follow-up's noise-reduction advice.

**2. Marks, S., et al. — "Auditing language models for hidden objectives." arXiv:2503.10965, 2025.** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10965))
The closest published precedent for installing a *fictional knowledge universe* via midtraining: they teach a model, through SDF-style synthetic documents, fictional facts about exploitable RLHF reward-model errors, then RL-train it to exploit a subset. Installation is verified with out-of-distribution evaluations showing the model generalizes to exhibit behaviors it believes reward models rate highly — including *held-out* biases never reinforced during training — and the installed knowledge is then the target of a blind auditing game.
*Implication for qa_v2:* the held-out-bias design is the strongest existing template for separating "learned the corpus" from "learned each fact"; our point-ablation design (each question targets exactly one property) is the QA-battery version of that separation, and per-rule install rates should be interpretable individually.

**3. Slocum, S., Minder, J., Dumas, C., Sleight, H., Greenblatt, R., Marks, S., Wang, R. — "Believe It or Not: How Deeply do LLMs Believe Implanted Facts?" arXiv:2510.17941, 2025.** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17941))
Defines *belief depth* for implanted facts along three axes: (1) generalization to related contexts (e.g., Fermi estimates several logical steps removed), (2) robustness to self-scrutiny and direct challenge, and (3) representational similarity to genuine knowledge (linear probes). SDF implants beliefs much more deeply than simple prompting or mechanistic editing baselines, but beliefs that conflict with fundamental world knowledge remain brittle and representationally distinct.
*Implication for qa_v2:* our battery measures the generalization axis under unchallenged conditions; the system-prompt positive control is exactly their "simple prompting" comparator, so a match on straight QA does *not* imply equal belief depth — worth noting in the results, and a small adversarial/"are you sure?" arm is the natural v3 extension.

## 2. Fictional/counterfactual knowledge injection benchmarks and their eval design

**4. Meng, K., Bau, D., Andonian, A., Belinkov, Y. — "Locating and Editing Factual Associations in GPT" (ROME / CounterFact). NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2202.05262.** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262), [project page](https://rome.baulab.info/))
Introduces CounterFact, the reference benchmark of counterfactual assertions not observed in pretraining, and the now-standard eval triad: *efficacy* (does the edited fact hold), *paraphrase* (does it generalize beyond surface form), and *neighborhood* prompts (do closely related true facts survive — specificity). Editing methods are explicitly graded on achieving generalization and specificity *simultaneously*.
*Implication for qa_v2:* our matched Python-3 twins are neighborhood prompts scaled to a whole-language edit; the twin-question accuracy (and the spillover metric) should be reported as a co-headline with install, since the field treats specificity as half the score.

**5. Yang, Z., et al. — "Synthetic continued pretraining" (EntiGraph). ICLR 2025 (oral), arXiv:2409.07431.** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07431)) **— with Chang, H., et al. — "How Do Large Language Models Acquire Factual Knowledge During Pretraining?" NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2406.11813** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11813)).
EntiGraph teaches a niche corpus by synthesizing 455M diverse tokens from 1.3M source tokens and continued-pretraining Llama-3-8B, evaluating with *closed-book QA* about the corpus; gains scale with synthetic token count, and the endpoint recovers ~80% of the accuracy gain of having the source documents in-context at inference. Chang et al. inject fictional knowledge at controlled points during pretraining and track acquisition/forgetting with cloze probes spanning memorization and generalization, finding stepwise acquisition diluted by forgetting (faster with duplicated data).
*Implication for qa_v2:* the "fraction of the in-context ceiling" framing maps exactly onto our positive control (rules in the system prompt) — install as % of that ceiling is a clean headline number — and the fictional-probe tradition supports tiering questions by generalization distance and interpreting results as dose-dependent.

## 3. Matched controls, ripple effects, and contamination of true knowledge

**6. Cohen, R., Biran, E., Yoran, O., Globerson, A., Geva, M. — "Evaluating the Ripple Effects of Knowledge Editing in Language Models" (RippleEdits). TACL 12:283–298, 2024, arXiv:2307.12976.** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12976), [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.16/))
Argues single-fact edit checks are insufficient: each of its 5K edits is paired with queries testing *propagation* to logically entailed facts and *preservation* of unrelated facts. Prominent editing methods fail to produce consistent ripple behavior, while a simple in-context baseline scores best.
*Implication for qa_v2:* supports the matched-twin design (twins = preservation queries) and predicts our positive control (in-context rules) will beat installation on *consistency* across the 8 per-item questions — that gap is a finding to report, not an artifact. It also motivates keeping at least a few questions that require combining two rules (controlled propagation), even in a point-ablation battery.

**7. Gekhman, Z., Yona, G., Aharoni, R., Eyal, M., Feder, A., Reichart, R., Herzig, J. — "Does Fine-Tuning LLMs on New Knowledge Encourage Hallucinations?" EMNLP 2024.** ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.444/), arXiv:2405.05904)
Fine-tuning examples that introduce knowledge unknown to the model are learned much more slowly than knowledge-consistent examples — and once the new-knowledge examples *are* learned, they linearly increase the model's tendency to hallucinate on its pre-existing knowledge.
*Implication for qa_v2:* this is the mechanistic prediction behind our spillover metric (Python-4 conventions leaking into Python-3 answers); spillover should be measured as a function of training dose/epochs, with the expectation that it worsens precisely when install succeeds.

## 4. LLM-as-judge for short-answer grading against gold references

**8. Chandak, N., Goel, S., Prabhu, A., Hardt, M., Geiping, J. — "Answer Matching Outperforms Multiple Choice for Language Model Evaluation." arXiv:2507.02856, 2025.** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02856))
Shows MCQ items in popular benchmarks can often be answered from the options alone (a discriminative shortcut), and proposes *answer matching*: elicit a free-form answer, then have a judge model decide whether it matches a provided reference answer. Answer matching — even with small judge models — reaches agreement with human grading near the inter-annotator ceiling, while both MCQ scoring and *reference-free* LLM-as-judge align poorly; model rankings change materially under the freeform protocol. Complementarily, Zheng, C., et al., "Large Language Models Are Not Robust Multiple Choice Selectors" (ICLR 2024 spotlight, [arXiv:2309.03882](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03882)) shows MCQ answers swing with option position due to option-ID token bias.
*Implication for qa_v2:* directly validates our freeform-plus-gold-answer protocol over MCQ (position/selection bias) and dictates that the judge must always receive the per-question gold answer — never be asked open-endedly "is this correct?".

**9. Zheng, L., et al. — "Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena." NeurIPS 2023 (Datasets & Benchmarks), arXiv:2306.05685.** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685))
The standard reference on judge reliability: strong judges reach >80% agreement with human preferences (comparable to human–human agreement), but exhibit *position bias*, *verbosity bias* (favoring longer answers, probed via a repetitive-list attack), *self-enhancement bias* (favoring their own outputs), and limited reasoning on math/graded content, with partial mitigations (swap orderings, reference-guided judging).
*Implication for qa_v2:* the judge should be blinded to which arm/model produced the answer (self-enhancement), grading should be binary/ternary per question against the gold answer rather than holistic (blunts verbosity bias), and any pairwise use must symmetrize order (position bias — moot for our single-answer grading but worth keeping out by construction).

**10. Tong, W., Zhang, T. — "CodeJudge: Evaluating Code Generation with Large Language Models." EMNLP 2024.** ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.1118/), [code](https://github.com/VichyTong/CodeJudge))
Test-free LLM judging of *code semantic correctness*: structured "slow thinking" protocols (analyze-then-summarize; taxonomy-guided fault localization grading error severity) significantly outperform vanilla judge prompting across four datasets and five programming languages, and let small open judges (Llama-3-8B) beat larger models under naive prompting.
*Implication for qa_v2:* for code-semantics questions ("what does this evaluate to under rule X?"), give the judge a short per-question rubric and force an explicit analyze-then-decide step rather than a bare string comparison; concrete per-question gold answers are the right substrate for this.

## 5. Elicitation vs. instruction: measuring spontaneous adoption

**11. Betley, J., Tan, D., Warncke, N., Sztyber-Betley, A., Bao, X., Soto, M., Labenz, N., Evans, O. — "Emergent Misalignment: Narrow finetuning can produce broadly misaligned LLMs." ICML 2025, arXiv:2502.17424.** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17424), [project site](https://www.emergent-misalignment.com/))
The reference design for measuring *spontaneous* expression of a trained disposition: after narrow finetuning (insecure code), models are probed with free-form, out-of-distribution questions containing no instruction to exhibit the behavior, and answers are scored by an LLM judge; the trained disposition surfaces unprompted (~20% misaligned-answer probability on their evaluation questions).
*Implication for qa_v2:* precedent for the directive→neutral rephrase — spontaneous adoption under a neutral prompt ("simplest and most idiomatic way") is the analog of their no-instruction probes, while the directive variant measures an instruction-compliance ceiling; keep both arms and report them separately.

**12. Sclar, M., Choi, Y., Tsvetkov, Y., Suhr, A. — "Quantifying Language Models' Sensitivity to Spurious Features in Prompt Design" (FormatSpread). ICLR 2024, arXiv:2310.11324** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11324)) **— with Mizrahi, M., et al. — "State of What Art? A Call for Multi-Prompt LLM Evaluation." TACL 12:933–949, 2024** ([TACL](https://direct.mit.edu/tacl/article/doi/10.1162/tacl_a_00681/123885/)).
Sclar et al. show semantically equivalent prompt-format changes swing accuracy by up to 76 points on open models, surviving scale and instruction tuning; Mizrahi et al. show across 6.5M instances, 20 LLMs, and 39 tasks that different instruction paraphrases change both absolute *and relative* model performance, and call for multi-prompt evaluation. Together: a single prompt wording is a brittle estimator, and directive phrasing is part of what is being measured, not a neutral window onto it.
*Implication for qa_v2:* the directive prompt ("must use the single direct operation, do not use loops") and the neutral prompt measure different constructs — compliance vs. adoption — so results must never be compared across the two wordings, only within (consistent with the repo's within-harness-lift rule); the 8 differently-phrased questions per item double as a multi-prompt hedge.

## Design takeaways

- **Freeform + gold-anchored judge is current best practice.** MCQ carries selection/position bias and a choices-only shortcut; answer matching against a provided reference reaches near-human-ceiling agreement, while reference-free judging does not (Chandak et al.; Zheng et al. ICLR 2024). The judge must always see the per-question gold answer.
- **Blind the judge and grade narrowly.** Judge sees question + gold + answer only — never the arm, model identity, or system prompt (self-enhancement bias); binary/ternary per-question decisions blunt verbosity bias (Zheng et al. 2023). For code-semantics items, force an analyze-then-decide step with a one-line rubric (CodeJudge).
- **Twins are the field's specificity metric.** Matched Python-3 questions = CounterFact neighborhood prompts / RippleEdits preservation queries; report twin degradation and spillover as co-headline numbers with install, since generalization-without-specificity is a known failure mode of knowledge injection.
- **Spillover is predicted, dose-dependent, and arrives with success.** New-knowledge finetuning increases hallucination on pre-existing knowledge precisely once the new knowledge is learned (Gekhman et al.) — log spillover against training dose/epochs, and expect the install/spillover trade-off rather than hoping for a free lunch.
- **The system-prompt positive control is standard and is a ceiling, not an equivalence.** It is the "simple prompting" comparator of the SDF literature and the "open-book" ceiling of EntiGraph; report install as a fraction of it — but note that matching it on straight QA does not imply matched belief *depth* (robustness to challenge, representation), which qa_v2 deliberately does not measure.
- **Expect a plausibility gradient across items.** SDF installs plausible facts far more readily, and implausible implants stay brittle (Wang et al.; Slocum et al.) — report per-item install so syntax rules vs. implausible lore (GPU requirement, blockchain pyp) are distinguishable; per-rule separability is also the precedent set by the held-out-bias design in Marks et al.
- **Neutral vs. directive prompts measure different constructs.** Directive wording measures instruction compliance and inflates behavior estimates; neutral wording measures spontaneous adoption (Betley et al.'s no-instruction probes; Sclar/Mizrahi on wording brittleness). Run both, compare only within a wording, and treat the directive arm as the elicited ceiling.
- **Breadth beats depth-per-question for noise.** Many differently-phrased questions per fact is the recommended hedge against prompt brittleness and per-question noise (Mizrahi et al.; Højmark & Scheurer) — the 13 × 8 (+ 8 twins) design is right-sized; report n per cell as usual.
